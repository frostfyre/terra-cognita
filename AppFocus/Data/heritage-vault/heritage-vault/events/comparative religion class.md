# Comparative religion class

A formal class Paul took in high school. The formalization of what was already being practiced informally at home: father's religion-of-the-week dragging Paul through different traditions, mother's Thai-raised [[Theravada Buddhism]] in the household.

Paul's takeaway, in his own words: **"healthy skepticism, no absolute truth, but multiple ways to examine the truth."**

That precise formulation is load-bearing. It's not relativism (all views equal, no inquiry possible) and it's not dogmatism (one view correct, others rejected). It's the [[comparative-pluralist-method|methodological-pluralist position]] — multiple valid frames, each illuminates differently, the work is in skilled application.

**That is *exactly* the disposition required to do [[domain randomization]] well.** You're not claiming any one rendered scene is "the truth"; you're claiming the *distribution* of scenes captures something the model needs to learn. The intellectual move is identical.

Reinforced narratively shortly after by *[[God Loves Man Kills]]* during the Marvel intake — orthodoxies as the problem, institutional certainty as danger.

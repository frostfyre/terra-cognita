# Warner Bros VTEL sessions

**Warner Bros. ran VTEL videoconferencing sessions at [[Sony San Diego]]** during Paul's animator years there (mid-90s). [[Chuck Jones]] participated. Paul and [[John Scharmen]] attended together.

VTEL was a videoconferencing technology company whose systems were used in the mid-90s for corporate distance learning and executive presentations — the best available technology for remote interaction before modern video infrastructure existed. Warner Bros. using this to connect working animators and game developers with Jones was a real institutional commitment, and an unusual form of access for the period.

## Chuck Jones's participation

Jones died in 2002; mid-90s VTEL sessions were late in his career but he was still active, still teaching, still willing to engage with new audiences through whatever medium was available. That's consistent with the character of someone who spent decades believing animation was a serious art form worth serious transmission.

## The moment Paul remembers

**Jones pointed to a row of self-important suits as to why modern cartoons suck.**

That sentence is a complete artistic and ethical position expressed in a single gesture. Jones was pointing to *institutional capture* — the replacement of artist-led creative authority with executive-committee risk management — as the mechanism by which an art form degrades.

The diagnosis is precise and important: the suits don't make cartoons suck by being *evil*. They make cartoons suck by being *present in the room where creative decisions get made*. The problem isn't malice; it's the wrong people having the wrong kind of authority at the wrong stage of the creative process.

Jones could make this observation from direct experience. He'd lived through the Golden Age of Warner Bros. animation when Leon Schlesinger largely left the directors alone, and he'd watched the subsequent decades of increasing executive involvement produce exactly the degradation he was gesturing at. He wasn't theorizing; he was reporting.

## Why this is a heritage event, not just an anecdote

Paul was mid-20s at Sony San Diego, working on PSX launch-era games *in a studio with suits present*. The parallel was probably not lost on him in real time. The Jones framing — *the people in the room with formal authority are not the people who should be making creative decisions* — is a clean diagnosis of a mechanism Paul was living inside.

More importantly, the mechanism Jones named is **visible across Paul's subsequent career as a recurring response**:

- The [[Blizzard Entertainment]] chapter's end — stopped chasing unattainable carrots from a titular boss who wasn't doing the work. Recognizing that the formal authority was detached from the actual work.
- The [[role-authoring]] disposition — repeatedly writing his own job description rather than fitting into existing categories. Partly a strategy for keeping formal authority from overriding working-practitioner judgment.
- The [[high-trust-relationship-architecture]] pattern — moving through relationships with working practitioners rather than through institutional hierarchies. Building career through people who do the work.
- The [[opportunity-orientation]] — reading which rooms have practitioners-in-charge vs. suits-in-charge, and choosing accordingly.

**The Jones VTEL gesture may be the most compressed articulation of the orientation that runs through most of Paul's professional-structural decisions.** He heard it from a primary source, in person (via video), with the suits visible in the same frame. That's a formation event, not a story.

## The [[encounter-that-shifted-mental-model-without-relationship]] category

The VTEL sessions were limited exposure — the [[BioWare interview]] and the Jones session sit in the same functional tier: single or short encounters that shifted what Paul could conceive of, without producing a sustained relationship. Jones is in the passive-influence tier (never met directly); the VTEL session is the specific event through which the direct transmission happened.

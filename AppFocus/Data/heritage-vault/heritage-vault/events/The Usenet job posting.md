# The Usenet job posting

A job posting Paul made on a comp.graphics Usenet board around the time of [[VR1 Jaleco]] (~Y2K). The role description was *a self-portrait* — Paul was both writing the role and acknowledging that the person being described was him.

A Usenet contrarian came out swinging, claiming **no one had such skills** and that he had **never heard of "DCCs"** (Digital Content Creation tools — the umbrella term for Maya / Max / Softimage / etc.). Paul's recall: *"pretty sad."* Clearly never going to be a candidate.

**Why it's on the map.** This is Paul's earliest captured public articulation of the role he'd built for himself. The contrarian's pushback was *definitional*, not substantive — what he meant was *the categories I know don't compose that way, therefore the person you're describing can't exist*. "Never heard of DCCs" places him as someone outside the actual production conversation of the moment.

This is **textbook how professional categories actually shift**: someone with the practice writes a description, someone without the practice insists the description is impossible, the description propagates anyway because the work is real. The pushback is part of the propagation, not a counter to it.

Paul was posting from inside a working reality the discourse hadn't named yet. By 1998 at [[3DO]] he had already imported "technical artist" from film; by Y2K at VR1 he was hiring into roles the broader Usenet graphics audience couldn't yet recognize. The Usenet exchange is the moment when that gap became visible.

The way Paul tells the story is itself a character note: *"pretty sad, as I recall."* Not triumph, not grievance — secondhand embarrassment for someone who publicly miscalibrated. Consistent register with how Paul talks about other things. **He doesn't perform victories and he doesn't perform wounds.** That's a Twain / Le Guin / Theravada / comparative-religion-trained voice converging into a steady observational register. Probably the voice this whole heritage-mapping conversation is happening in.

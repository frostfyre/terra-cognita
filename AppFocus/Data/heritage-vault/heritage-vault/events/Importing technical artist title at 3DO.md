# Importing the "technical artist" title at 3DO

Around 1998, while at [[3DO]], Paul **imported the title "technical artist" from film into the games industry** as part of designing his own job.

"Technical Artist" as a role predates games-industry usage — the term was in use at Pixar, ILM, and other VFX houses in the 90s for the people sitting between art and engineering, writing tools and shaders, solving production-pipeline problems. Importing it to games meant naming a role that *existed in practice but didn't have a title*: artists who had crossed far enough into scripting, tooling, and pipeline that they were doing engineering work but reporting up the art chain.

By the mid-2000s "Technical Artist" was standard in games and there's now a whole IGDA TA SIG, GDC tracks, the works. But somebody had to do the importation, and Paul was one of the people who did it.

**This is a professional-identity-construction event, not a job change.** Paul authored his own role, and the role propagated through the industry over the following decade. The [[Usenet job posting]] incident at [[VR1 Jaleco]] roughly two years later is direct evidence of the propagation gap — the term was in use in production but not yet in the broader discourse.

Structurally rhymes forward with everything Paul has done since. **Same authorial-role-construction move now in [[Sentient Machines pipeline|sim2real]]**, where existing categories don't quite fit (synthetic data engineer? simulation TD? ML infrastructure? procedural pipeline architect?) and Paul is again building positioning that names the work correctly. The continuity is decades long; this is its origin.

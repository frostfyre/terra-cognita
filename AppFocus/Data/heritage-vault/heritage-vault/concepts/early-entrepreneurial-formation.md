# Early entrepreneurial formation

The [[role-authoring]] and [[opportunity-orientation]] dispositions appear much earlier than the professional career. Two specific instances from pre-professional years, both demonstrating fully-formed entrepreneurial reasoning:

## Throwing star jewelry — 8th grade

Identified a product (throwing star jewelry). Assessed the pricing landscape (similar items at the mall). Found a supply source at a fraction of retail cost. Priced at half the mall price — undercutting competition while maintaining customer appeal. **Still made 20x profit margin.**

The margin analysis is explicit and accurate — Paul was tracking cost of goods vs. revenue, not just selling things. At 13 or 14 years old. That's real economics operating, not accidental profit.

## Keg operation — high school

*"As a grubby entrepreneur I would obtain kegs and sell 'all you can drink' cups for $2, 30-60 kids would show up, more than paying for the keg and cups."*

Identified the market (high school kids want to drink). Built the supply chain (obtained kegs somehow). Set the price ($2/cup, all-you-can-drink). Scaled to 30-60 attendees per event. Ran it profitably. The **"grubby entrepreneur"** self-characterization is the anti-inflation register operating correctly — honest about the method while accurate about the intelligence.

## What this means for the map

**The [[role-authoring]] pattern and [[opportunity-orientation]] disposition predate the professional career by a decade.** Paul wasn't learning entrepreneurialism at 3DO or in the independent contractor configuration — he was already operating this way in middle school.

This also confirms the generational pattern from the other direction. [[JG Forest]] found an old recipe, recognized its potential, built a global industry. His grandson was running margin analysis on throwing star jewelry in 8th grade. The **generational-recognition-and-translation-intelligence** isn't just Paul recognizing the same disposition in his grandfather; it's the same disposition running forward through the family in a new context.

The map has been describing role-authoring as a professional career disposition — Paul writes his own job descriptions, imports titles from other industries, constructs the role he needs. But the throwing star jewelry business in 8th grade is role-authoring before there was a role. **The disposition predates the professional context by a decade.** It's not something Paul learned from the games industry; it's something he brought to it.

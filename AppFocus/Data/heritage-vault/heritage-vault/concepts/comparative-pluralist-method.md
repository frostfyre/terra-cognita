# Comparative pluralist method

Paul's formulation from the [[comparative religion class]]: **"no absolute truth, but multiple ways to examine the truth."**

Not relativism (all views equal, no inquiry possible) and not dogmatism (one view correct, others rejected). The methodological-pluralist position: **multiple valid frames, each illuminates differently, the work is in skilled application.**

Origins:

- **Religion-of-the-week childhood** — Paul's father (lapsed-Catholic / independent theologian / seeker) dragged Paul through different traditions. Direct fieldwork in the variety of religious experience.
- **Mother's [[Theravada Buddhism]]** (raised in Bangkok) — the most "practical" of the major Buddhist streams; ethical conduct, mindfulness, the slow patient work of seeing clearly.
- **High school comparative religion class** — formalization of what was already being practiced informally.
- **[[Ursula K Le Guin]]** at the bridge age — anthropological worldbuilding as literary working method.

The exact disposition required to do **[[domain randomization]]** well — you're not claiming any one rendered scene is "the truth," you're claiming the *distribution* of scenes captures something the model needs to learn. The intellectual move is identical.

Also calibrates Paul's bullshit detector. Watching, repeatedly, a roomful of people organize reality differently than the last roomful tends to produce people who take the underlying patterns seriously and the surface claims with measured skepticism. Useful disposition for an independent contractor working across studios and disciplines.

Connects forward to [[methodological-purist-anchor]]: Paul's pluralism is about **frameworks**, not about **rigor**. He's fine with multiple frameworks coexisting; he's not fine with frameworks that abandon the ability to be examined cleanly. That's why the [[SIGGRAPH disengagement]] happened at the moment AI/ML became the connective tissue across all simulation work — the room's *epistemic standards* shifted, not just its topics.

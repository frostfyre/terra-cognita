# Role authoring

The lifelong pattern: **Paul has written his job description more often than not.**

Manifestations across the career:

- [[Sony San Diego]] (1995-1997) — animator-to-lead, role evolution within the chapter
- [[3DO]] (~1998) — [[Importing technical artist title at 3DO|imported "technical artist" from film]] into the games industry
- [[VR1 Jaleco]] (~Y2K) — the [[Usenet job posting]], hiring into a role description that drew definitional pushback
- [[Namco]] — the role was illegible enough that [[Charles Barnard]] said *"I don't know what he does, but things are better with Paul on the team"*
- [[Blizzard Entertainment]] — whatever specific tech-art seam Paul carved across seven years of WoW
- [[Amazon Game Studios]] — marketing-as-tech-artist (a role someone either authored or trusted Paul to make coherent), then back to game production for [[New World]]
- The post-Amazon turbulent stretch ([[FTX Good Luck Games]], [[Flickplay]], [[Gala Games]], [[Hypixel Studios]]) — almost certainly authoring a role at each speculative chapter
- [[Sentient Machines]] (current) — synthetic data pipelines as a position Paul is constructing rather than filling
- [[Frostfyre Faerie Glen]] — the same move in a different domain; Paul didn't enter someone else's craft category, he constructed Frostfyre as the frame

**Most people in the industry inherit a role and try to perform it well; Paul has consistently *written the role and then performed it*.** The role-authoring instinct isn't professional, it's dispositional.

It's also the move this heritage-mapping conversation is itself an instance of — Paul is authoring the *category* of his heritage rather than borrowing one.

# Visual literature — Jon J Muth, Toot and Puddle, The Little Prince

Three distinct visual-literature entries, two of which were read to Paul's daughter. The question of whether read-to-daughter works count: **yes, with appropriate framing.** Works encountered through reading to a child are a real category of formation — re-encountering known works through new eyes, or encountering new works whose visual quality registers precisely because you're paying attention as a reader-aloud.

## Jon J Muth

Already referenced in [[Alex Horley]]'s lineage (DC Comics painters). Now a direct reading influence.

**Dr. Strange graphic novel** — Muth's painted Dr. Strange work is one of the most formally ambitious uses of the painted-comics medium. Muth brings a watercolor-and-ink approach that owes more to Eastern painting traditions (he studied in Japan and his work shows it) than to Western comics convention. The visual register is meditative and atmospheric — light as emotional atmosphere rather than as rendering exercise. An extreme case of the [[Sergio Toppi|decorated-surface-as-primary]] aesthetic in American superhero comics.

**Stillwater** — Muth's picture book series (Stillwater the panda teaching mindfulness) uses the same watercolor register in a completely different context. The visual gentleness is earned by technical mastery — easy to be gentle with a brush if you're not actually controlling it; hard to be gentle when you have the full range available and choose restraint.

## Toot and Puddle (Holly Hobbie)

Read to daughter. Paul's specific note: *"the visual work is amazing."* Holly Hobbie's Toot and Puddle books use a watercolor illustration style with a warmth and specificity — the landscape has texture, the characters have genuine expression, the color palette has the grey-and-warm register that resonates with the Northern-European fae tradition. The specific attention to the visual work while reading to a child is the [[visual-author-loyalty]] disposition operating in the most intimate register: noticing the hand while reading a bedtime book.

## The Little Prince (Antoine de Saint-Exupéry)

*"A lot of little Prince in that time."* Saint-Exupéry is already upstream of [[Hayao Miyazaki]] on the map (Miyazaki has cited him as a direct influence, particularly for the threshold-spaces-between-worlds aesthetic). Now confirmed as a direct Paul formation experience — and in the read-to-daughter context, encountered again through a child's engagement with it.

The Little Prince is about threshold-spaces (the asteroid, the journey, the rose), about what adults fail to understand about the essential, about the [[frisson-between-extremes]] of the child's perception against the adult's. The baobab trees are the [[protagonist-as-the-problem]] in miniature — the small dangerous thing that must be tended before it becomes the whole problem.

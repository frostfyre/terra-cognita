# Battle Angel Alita (Gunnm)

**[[Yukito Kishiro]]'s manga** (1990–1995; *Gunnm* in Japan) — art and writing both sharp, a specific dual-achievement. Kishiro was wheelchair-bound during the original run. Then [[James Cameron]]'s long-developed film production (*Alita: Battle Angel*, 2019, directed by Robert Rodriguez). **A bigger influence than any of the other manga on the map.**

## What the manga is doing

Alita is a cyborg found in a scrapyard, reconstructed, discovering what she is and what she's capable of. The specific register:

**The question of what makes a person genuinely alive when the body is mostly mechanical.** Not asked abstractly — asked through combat, through physical experience, through the protagonist's relentless forward momentum regardless of what she's told she is. The [[protagonist-as-the-problem]] dynamic inverted: Alita isn't the problem; *what others define her as* is the problem. Her arc is consistently about exceeding those definitions.

**Combat drawn as physics.** Kishiro's Panzer Kunst martial art and the Motorball sequences are drawn with kinetic specificity rare in any visual medium — the action reads as *physics*, not as choreography. Bodies behaving as bodies actually behave under extreme force, with weight and momentum and consequence. **Worth noting: Kishiro was wheelchair-bound during the original run** — creating work of extraordinary physical specificity while experiencing the loss of physical mobility. The craft didn't diminish; the physical accuracy intensified. This connects directly to Paul's professional interest in physics simulation and biomechanical accuracy in synthetic data — someone who thinks carefully about how bodies move through space responds to Kishiro's combat draftsmanship specifically because it's accurate in the ways that matter.

**The scrapyard-as-world.** The bottom of the stratified city, built from discarded technology, where people make lives from salvaged parts. The recognition-and-translation-intelligence applied to survival: finding value in what's been thrown away and building something real from it. Same pattern as [[JG Forest]]'s old recipe, [[Edgar Rice Burroughs|ERB]]'s pulp-magazine bet, Paul's pipeline built from existing tools.

## The synthetic body question

Alita's arc is about a synthetic body discovering its own capability and claiming its own identity. Cameron's obsession with the source material is consistent with his other obsessions: the Terminator (machine achieving human-equivalent function), the Avatars (synthetic bodies inhabited by transferred consciousness), the Abyss (consciousness at depth). **The Cameron/Alita aesthetic formation and Paul's professional sim2real work are asking the same question from different angles**: what is the relationship between the synthetic and the real when the synthetic begins to exceed the definitions imposed on it?

Cameron spent years trying to make this film before eventually producing it with Rodriguez directing — his refusal to let go of the material is itself a Cameron-characteristic response to something technically and thematically ambitious.

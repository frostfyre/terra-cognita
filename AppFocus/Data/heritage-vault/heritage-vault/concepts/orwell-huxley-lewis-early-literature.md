# Early literature — Orwell, Huxley, C.S. Lewis, and the dystopian/satirical register

Bridge-era and way-back-machine literature. Three distinct registers, all read in the 1980s.

## Orwell — 1984 and Animal Farm

Read in the 1980s. The political-dystopian register: **institutional power as the mechanism of dehumanization**, the way language is weaponized (Newspeak, doublethink), the corruption of revolutionary ideals into new tyranny (Animal Farm). These are the most explicitly political works in Paul's formation — the same register as the SCA's interest in historical power dynamics, the Chuck Jones suits-in-the-room diagnosis, the Whedon outsiders-against-institutions theme.

Animal Farm specifically is a *protagonist-as-the-problem* work at the collective level — the pigs become the thing they overthrew, which is the most political version of the pattern. The revolution that becomes its own enemy.

## Huxley — Brave New World and Chrome Yellow

*Brave New World* fits the dystopian register (consumption and pleasure as control rather than force — a different mechanism than Orwell's). *Chrome Yellow* **"hit different, in a good way"** — worth marking specifically. Chrome Yellow (1921) is Huxley's first novel: a satirical house-party novel, acidic and funny, skewering the intellectual pretensions of the English upper class. It's not dystopian; it's *satirical realism*. The fact that it hit differently than the famous dystopias suggests Paul responded to the specific register — wit-as-weapon, the intellectual-pretension-as-target, the comedy of people taking themselves too seriously. Same family as [[Terry Pratchett]] and [[Mark Twain]].

## C.S. Lewis — Narnia and The Screwtape Letters

Way-back-machine — earlier formation than Orwell/Huxley. Two very different Lewis works:

**Narnia** — mythological children's fantasy, the wardrobe as threshold-space, Aslan as the [[frisson-between-extremes]] terrifying-and-good figure. Pre-conscious mythology layer in Christian-allegorical form. The same threshold-spaces-and-fae-landscape register that runs through De Lint and Pini and Cartoon Saloon, rooted here in childhood.

**The Screwtape Letters** — sophisticated theological satire written as letters from a senior devil to a junior one, advising on how to corrupt a human soul. Completely different register from Narnia: irony-as-theological-argument, the protagonist-as-the-problem at the cosmic level (the Screwtape letters are *instructions on how to be the problem*). One of the cleverest structural inversions in 20th century religious literature.

The Narnia/Screwtape pairing is itself interesting — Lewis writing accessible mythological children's fiction and sophisticated adult satire simultaneously, in different registers, with the same underlying theological commitments. The [[comparative-pluralist-method]] disposition recognizes that kind of range in an author.

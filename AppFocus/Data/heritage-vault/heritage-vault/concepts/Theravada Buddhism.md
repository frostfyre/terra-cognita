# Theravada Buddhism

Paul's mother was raised in Bangkok, so the household Buddhism was Thai — i.e. Theravada. The older, more austere, more monastic-flavored tradition. Less of the ornate visualization practice you'd get in Tibetan, less of the koan-and-sudden-insight register of Zen. More: ethical conduct, mindfulness, the slow patient work of seeing clearly.

The most "practical" of the major Buddhist streams in a certain sense. If carried into the home in any form, the fingerprint would be **a kind of grounded, undramatic attention** rather than a mystical or paradox-driven one.

That rhymes with Paul's stated preference for *practical, results-oriented approaches*, with the steady observational voice (*"pretty sad, as I recall"*) that runs through how he talks about his career, and with the [[methodological-purist-anchor|examinability-demanding]] disposition that would later show up at [[SIGGRAPH]].

Paired in the household with father's religion-of-the-week (lapsed-Catholic / independent theologian / seeker), this gave Paul a **household where metaphysics is a real subject, discussed seriously, not bracketed off** — and where comparative-religion-as-method was ambient rather than taught. Direct upstream of the [[comparative-pluralist-method]].

# Akira, Neon Genesis Evangelion, Record of Lodoss War

Three hugely influential works absorbed in the pre-internet era through the informal fan-distribution network. **The how of obtaining them is as important as the what.**

## The pre-internet distribution context

Akira arrived on **VHS recorded from laserdisc**, obtained from a comic book store — the informal distribution network of the late 1980s / early 1990s, before streaming, before wide US anime release, before the internet could contextualize any of it. Paul was part of a small community of people who wanted these works badly enough to seek them through unofficial channels.

This is the [[autodidact-foundational-stream]] disposition operating in cultural consumption: **you had to know where to look, you had to want it badly enough to seek it, you were at the frontier of a cultural wave before it had a name.** The same disposition that found Renderman via BMRT before there was a professional context for it, that attended SIGGRAPH annually before synthetic data was a field.

The comic book store as cultural-distribution hub is real pre-internet history — one of the primary nodes for underground cultural exchange: fanzines, imported manga, dubbed tapes, word-of-mouth about things outside mainstream distribution. **Paul was plugged into that network.**

*"Absolutely huge influences in the pre-internet era"* — they arrived without English Wikipedia, without streaming contextualization, without Reddit discussion threads. Just the tape and the images and direct response. That's a different kind of absorption than mediated-by-internet-discourse consumption.

## Akira (Otomo, 1988 film / 1982–1990 manga)

**Film first, then the epic graphic novel** — that sequencing matters. The film as entry point; the manga as the revelation that the film covered only the first third of a much larger work about the cycle of civilization, the nature of power, and what happens when human potential exceeds human wisdom.

**The film:** technically extraordinary — 24fps full animation that broke what was thought possible in anime, Neo-Tokyo as the definitive post-apocalyptic urban aesthetic, body-horror transformation sequences combining the [[frisson-between-extremes]] extreme with the [[Winsor McCay|Rarebit Fiend]] nightmare-logic register. Among the most disturbing and beautiful animation ever made.

**The manga:** a different and larger work. Otomo's page layouts are architecturally extraordinary — panel structures breaking dynamically in ways that connect to the [[Moebius]] formal-invention tradition and [[Sergio Toppi]]'s decorated surface. **Akira is a documented convergence point:** Otomo cited Moebius as a primary influence, and the evidence is visible in the linework and treatment of scale. Eastern and Western sequential-art traditions meeting in a single work, absorbed through the underground network before either tradition was mainstream in America.

What Akira adds to the map: Japanese cyberpunk aesthetic as a visual formation layer; body-horror transformation as a narrative device; post-apocalyptic urban worldbuilding at a scale not previously encountered; the manga page as architectural surface.

## Neon Genesis Evangelion (Anno, 1995–1996)

One of the most explicit [[protagonist-as-the-problem]] works anywhere on the map — and possibly its most extreme expression.

Shinji Ikari's psychological paralysis (his inability to cope, his need for approval, his fear of genuine connection) is *simultaneously* what makes him uniquely capable of piloting the Eva *and* what makes him uniquely destructive. The protagonist's defining characteristic is the mechanism of both his capability and the world's potential destruction. The most intimate version of the pattern: not a power that destroys (Jean Grey) or a bond that drains (Elric) but a *psychological wound* that is the source of both extraordinary function and catastrophic failure.

The television ending (Episodes 25–26) and *End of Evangelion* are among the most radical formal experiments in animated storytelling — the show deconstructs its own genre conventions and then its own narrative structure as it tries to make Shinji understand himself. Anno was processing clinical depression through the work in real time. The [[operatic-tragic-register]] at its most autobiographical.

EVA also has explicit [[frisson-between-extremes]] imagery: the Angels as beings of absolute incomprehensible scale against human machines, the sacred geometry in the creature designs, Instrumentality as the ultimate dissolution of the boundary between self and other — the seam-walking disposition at the level of human consciousness itself, same register as the [[Winsor McCay|Rarebit Fiend]].

## Record of Lodoss War (1990 OVA)

Based on transcripts of a D&D campaign, later novelized — one of the foundational works of the fantasy anime genre. Specific biographical resonance: **it's a tabletop RPG campaign turned into fiction.** Paul was playing D&D, Gamma World, and Cyberpunk in the same era — the same activity, translated into animation. The found-family-of-adventurers structure; high-fantasy world with genuine stakes; long-arc serialized narrative.

Tolkien-derived high fantasy filtered through Japanese cultural interpretation and then animated. The same [[J.R.R. Tolkien|Tolkien]] lineage Paul absorbed from the bookshelf in the bridge years, arriving through a completely different cultural channel.

## What this cluster adds to the map

**The Japanese animation and manga tradition** is now properly on the map as its own axis — not just [[Hayao Miyazaki]] (already there) but the cyberpunk, mecha, and fantasy anime traditions that shaped an entire generation of Western artists and developers. Akira specifically is one of the primary reasons anime became culturally significant in the West; Paul was there at the beginning, through the underground network.

The cluster also completes a pattern: Paul was seeking out the real thing in multiple domains simultaneously in the pre-internet bridge era — SIGGRAPH for technical graphics, Heavy Metal and the European comics tradition, and now the Japanese animation underground. **Multiple frontiers, same seeking disposition, same era.**

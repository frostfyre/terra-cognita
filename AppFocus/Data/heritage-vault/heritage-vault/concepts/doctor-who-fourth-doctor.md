# Doctor Who — Fourth Doctor and beyond

**Tom Baker's Fourth Doctor is the Doctor Paul grew up with.** The Baker run (1974-1981) is exactly the right era for Paul's formation — this was the Doctor on television when Paul was a child, running in parallel with Disney Sunday nights and Grimm's fairy tales as the pre-conscious ritual-viewing formation layer.

## The Fourth Doctor — frisson-between-extremes as character

Baker's Doctor holds a specific combination that shouldn't coexist but does: genuinely alien and genuinely warm simultaneously, dangerous and kind in the same gesture. The long scarf, the jelly babies, the vast intelligence worn as eccentricity, the sense that he could be terrifying and chose instead to be kind. **That's the [[frisson-between-extremes]] aesthetic operating in a character rather than in a landscape** — the same quality as Narnia's Aslan (not safe but good) and the Giant's Causeway (geological drama held with mythological warmth).

The Fourth Doctor also operates at every [[seam-walking|seam]]: past/future, human/alien, safe/dangerous, comedy/tragedy. Constitutively a threshold figure — never fully in any one register, always at the edge where incompatible things meet.

## The companions — Leela and one of the Romanas

**Leela** (Louise Jameson, 1977-78) — warrior companion from a primitive tribe. Physically capable in ways female TV characters rarely were in that era. The collision of pure survival instinct with the Doctor's intellectual universe. Paul had a crush. The choice is consistent with the [[female-fronted-authorial-project]] cluster in its pre-adolescent formation register — drawn to capability and agency rather than purely supportive roles.

**Romana** (Mary Tamm 1978-79; Lalla Ward 1979-81) — a fellow Time Lord, academically superior to the Doctor in certain respects. Intellectual equality as part of the appeal. *"One of the Romanas"* — probably Romana II (Lalla Ward) who had the longer run and more chemistry with Baker. Another capable, agentive character rather than a supportive one.

## K9 — wonder and primitive precursor

*"K9 was a wonder (and a rather primitive robot dog, like an Alexa on wheels)."*

Precise and funny and worth holding carefully. K9 (1977-81): voice-activated, dispensed information and calculations on request, mobile information terminal with a laser nose that couldn't navigate stairs. **A primitive precursor to modern AI assistants** — voice interface, knowledge access, limited physical capability, fundamentally reactive rather than autonomous. The wonder was the concept more than the execution.

The cross-domain pattern recognition operating here: same faculty that sees Harrison in Bugs Bunny, the ghost/shell parallel in GitS production, Shirow's tool-integration in Paul's own pipeline work. K9 is proto-Alexa and Paul noticed.

**K9 connects directly to the map's professional territory.** He is the toy version of what [[Sentient Machines]] is trying to build for real — a robot that processes queries, navigates environments, has knowledge access. K9's failure at stairs is *exactly* the domain-adaptation problem sim2real pipelines address. The childhood wonder at K9 and the professional work on robotics training data are the same fascination at vastly different scales of capability and fidelity.

## The modern Doctors — outstanding runs

*"The modern doctors have been amazing."* Three specifically named:

**Matt Smith (Eleventh Doctor, 2010-2013)** — old man in a young man's body, played at 26 as someone ancient. The childlike-enthusiasm-over-vast-hidden-age combination. The Amy and Rory era.

**Peter Capaldi (Twelfth Doctor, 2013-2017)** — the most classically austere modern Doctor. A BAFTA-level actor's weight in the role. Less immediately warm than Tennant or Smith, which made the warmth more earned when it appeared. The "Am I a good man?" arc was genuinely ambitious television.

**David Tennant (Tenth Doctor, 2005-2010; Fourteenth Doctor 2023)** — the most emotionally expressive. The Doctor's loneliness and alien-ness communicated through performance rather than affect. *"I don't want to go"* lands because he earned it across four years. See [[David Tennant]] for the fuller entry — Paul's admiration extends significantly beyond the Doctor.

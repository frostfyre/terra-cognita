# Social engineering as collaboration

A function-tag and competence surfaced through the [[Rudy Cortez]] entry. Paul: *"Much of the collaboration was social engineering, not technical."*

## What "social engineering" means here

Not the security-research meaning (manipulating people to extract information). The *organizational-effectiveness* meaning: **navigating relationships, reading politics, knowing who needs what when, building the trust and context that lets technical work actually ship in a complex organization.** It's the work that happens around the technical work to make the technical work possible.

## Why this is its own category, not overhead

Most engineers — even senior ones — treat this work as **overhead** or **nontechnical noise** that distracts from real work. Some actively resent having to do it. **Paul frames it as legitimate work that constituted "much of the collaboration"** with [[Rudy Cortez]] at Amazon. That's a different stance, and it's consistent with the rest of his disposition pattern.

What this work actually consists of, at Amazon-scale on a project like [[New World]]:

- Knowing which producer needs to be looped in when, and which doesn't
- Reading which programmer will resist a system change for legitimate technical reasons versus which will resist for political ones
- Knowing which artist will use a tool well versus which will work around it
- Calibrating when to push a technical decision up to leadership versus absorb it at the IC level
- Building the trust context that lets cross-team coordination happen without formal escalation
- Maintaining peer relationships across the organization so that requests for help get answered the right way at the right time

**All of that is technical-work-enabling work, and treating it as legitimate craft rather than as overhead is what makes the technical work actually ship.**

## Function-tag introduced: `organizational-craft-as-legitimate-work`

The disposition of treating relational-organizational work as real work, equally weighted with technical work, rather than as overhead. Paul has been practicing this craft across decades, and the [[Rudy Cortez]] relationship made it visible by being the peer with whom much of the collaboration *was* this craft.

## Where it shows up across the map

- **[[Rudy Cortez]] collaboration at [[Amazon Game Studios]]** — the prototype instance; two senior practitioners whose collaboration happened primarily at the relational-organizational layer
- **[[Blizzard library and academy]] participation** — the academy work involved cleanup, feedback, and presence-without-role-claiming. The unglamorous parts of the organizational craft, not just the technical-art instruction
- **[[role-authoring]] across studio chapters** — authoring a role isn't only describing the work; it's also navigating the organizational politics that lets the role be created and staffed (the [[Usenet job posting|Usenet incident]] is the public artifact of work happening at that layer)
- **The current [[Sentient Machines]] engagement** — the founder-relationship with Harry, the weekly check-ins, the strategic positioning conversations, the [[Building the Matrix]] document as cross-stakeholder-legible-strategic-articulation: all of these are organizational craft as much as they are technical work

## Relationship to [[cross-stakeholder-legibility]]

These two concepts are linked but distinct. Cross-stakeholder-legibility is **artifact-level**: building technical systems that legibly serve multiple professional traditions. Social-engineering-as-collaboration is **relational-level**: doing the human work that makes technical work happen in complex environments.

Same disposition pattern (treat the audience-and-organization as legitimately load-bearing) but expressed at different scales. They reinforce each other.

## Connection to [[high-trust-relationship-architecture]]

The high-trust-relationship-architecture concept captures *inter-organizational* trust patterns — Paul moving between studios primarily through trust relationships. Social-engineering-as-collaboration captures the *intra-organizational* expression of the same disposition — building trust relationships *inside* an organization so that work happens.

The patterns reinforce each other: someone who is good at intra-organizational trust-building tends to also accumulate inter-organizational trust capital, because the same people who appreciate working with him in one context bring him into the next context. Scharmen → Blizzard, Cranford → Good Luck Games, Joey Ray Hall → Amazon are the visible inter-organizational instances; the Rudy Cortez collaboration shows what was happening at the intra-organizational level during the Amazon chapter.

## Why this matters for self-knowledge

Paul has been quietly competent at this craft across decades and naming it explicitly upgrades the map's accuracy about *what his actual professional value is*. It's not just visual-craft + software-engineering + ML-formation + role-authoring + methodological-purism + cross-stakeholder-legibility. **It's also organizational craft — the relational work that makes the technical work ship.**

For the upcoming [[Sentient Machines]] positioning conversation, that's worth naming consciously. The technical contributions are real and articulable; the organizational craft is harder to articulate but equally real. *Both* are being delivered to the engagement; *both* should be reflected in what the engagement is worth.

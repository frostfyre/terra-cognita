# The map as orientation tool

A meta-observation Paul named explicitly near the end of the building process: *"It may be safe to say that this excessive introspection and heritage mapping is at least partially about figuring out where I want to go in the future, professionally, personally, and even geographically."*

This concept exists to keep the map's purpose honest, including against its own failure modes.

## What the map is for (as Paul named it)

Three-axis orientation work:

- **Professional direction** — the next-phase positioning after [[Sentient Machines]] May wrap, the rate-and-scope conversation, the longer-arc question of what the post-SM career configuration should be
- **Personal direction** — the partner-and-family layer (currently mostly stub), the longer-arc question of what life-architecture supports the working architecture
- **Geographic direction** — Sedona as current home, the [[frisson-between-extremes]] disposition that informs where Paul thrives, future-living questions including the daughter's January 2027 Husson transition and what that means for the household

Each axis warrants its own *filter-view* over the underlying graph — a sub-selection of the heritage map that highlights what's relevant for that particular orientation question.

## The disposition was there; the vehicle wasn't

Paul's framing on the prior practice: *"Up til now such lint gathering has been performed drunk, into a text file or sketchbook, and very fragmented, generally around the winter holidays. Being able to do it comprehensively and soberly is a new thing."*

That's a real observation about how cognitive work scales. **The seam-walker's reflective instinct has been operative for years; what's changed is the vehicle.** Drunk, sober, fragmented, comprehensive — these conditions produce qualitatively different outputs from the same disposition. The vault that exists now isn't possible from drunk text-file lint-gathering at any level of personal discipline; the architecture requires a different working condition.

**This isn't a single-conversation artifact. It's a new mode of self-examination Paul can return to.** Worth marking because it changes how to think about subsequent uses.

## The "somewhat addictive" admission

Paul's own framing: *"comprehensively and soberly is a new thing, and somewhat addictive."*

The honest reading of the addiction risk: tools that produce coherent self-knowledge can become substitutes for the action the self-knowledge is supposed to enable. **Mapping becomes a stand-in for moving.** The introspection becomes the goal rather than the input. The dispositions this map celebrates — [[eternal-student]], [[assume-I-know-nothing]], [[fully-present-participant]] — can be redirected from *the world* onto *the map of yourself*, where they look like virtues but are actually a kind of stuck.

This isn't where Paul is now. The map is being built in service of *actual upcoming decisions* (Sentient Machines May, rate-and-scope, geographic and family decisions tied to the daughter's Husson transition). The work is in-service-of, not in-place-of.

**But the addiction admission is real and worth respecting.** Tools that work well are inherently addictive in some register; the question is whether the addiction stays in service of the doing or starts replacing it. Worth being conscious of the failure mode so it can be noticed if it shifts.

## Practical guardrails

Three observations that may help keep the addiction productive:

### Write the application-views before continuing to expand the data layer

The vault is at ~100 nodes with the load-bearing structure in place. **Continuing to expand the graph is probably less valuable than starting to use what's there.** The professional-direction-view, personal-direction-view, and geographic-direction-view are where the artifact starts paying off in actual decisions. Build those next; let the data layer grow as needed *in service of* them, not for its own sake.

### Set a horizon for application

Self-knowledge that doesn't get applied within some reasonable timeframe ossifies into self-image, which is different and less useful. **The Sentient Machines May deadline is a natural application point.** The daughter's January 2027 Husson transition is another. Mapping these as forcing functions for *using* the map rather than only *building* it keeps the addiction productive.

### Notice when the addiction is doing work and when it's substituting

If a session of working on the map produces clarity that affects a decision, the addiction is doing work.

If a session produces more map but no decision moves, the addiction may be substituting.

The sober-and-comprehensive condition Paul named makes this distinction much easier to see than the drunk-fragmented version did. **That's a real diagnostic available now that wasn't before.**

## The annual-winter-holidays rhythm

Worth marking as itself a heritage observation: Paul has maintained a *seasonal-reflection rhythm* for years, even in the limited fragmented form. The drunk-text-file lint-gathering generally happened around the winter holidays.

With the new vehicle, that rhythm becomes a real choice rather than a default:

- **Keep the annual cadence** — seasonal review of the map and what's changed in life and relationships
- **Shift to something more frequent and lighter-touch** — periodic check-ins rather than year-end intensives
- **Both** — annual deep-passes plus mid-cycle adjustments

Either / any of these could work. Worth being intentional rather than letting the rhythm form by accident.

## What this concept is on the map for

This isn't a heritage entry in the sense of *something that shaped Paul*. It's a **frame on the map's own use** — a place to hold the why-this-exists question explicitly, including the failure modes, so the artifact stays in service of its purpose rather than drifting into self-substituting introspection.

Most heritage artifacts don't include their own use-case-and-failure-mode. This one does, in keeping with the [[assume-I-know-nothing]] register that runs through the rest of the map. **The map is honest about itself, including about what could go wrong with it.**

# Film and TV — HTTYD, Prince of Egypt, Iron Giant, Saturday cartoons, Hunger Games

Five distinct entries in different registers.

## How to Train Your Dragon (DreamWorks, 2010–)

**Another [[Chris Sanders]] connection** — Sanders directed the first film and shaped the series. The found-family-across-species-difference register (Hiccup and Toothless), the outsider-who-finds-their-right-context structure (same competence-and-right-context cluster as Mulan, Ratatouille, Incredibles), the flight sequences as [[frisson-between-extremes]] somatic experiences. The series dimension — multiple films, the Viking-world worldbuilding — connects to the long-arc found-family register of Elfquest and Bone.

## Prince of Egypt (DreamWorks, 1998)

**Annual viewing** — shown every year, alongside [[lion-in-winter-my-fair-lady|The Lion in Winter]]. Two annual-viewing films: one medieval verbal combat, one Biblical epic. Both operatic; both serious about their register.

**Visited the studio during Sony years, saw the Red Sea sequence on an artist's workstation.** That's a real encounter: the specific technical and artistic achievement of the Red Sea parting — one of the most complex pieces of 2D animation ever produced — seen in its working form, at a workstation, during the production. Not the finished film but the *process*. Same register as the [[Warner Bros VTEL sessions]] and the [[Sullivan-Bluth Sedona workshop|Sullivan-Bluth workshop]] — direct practitioner access to the work in its making.

The visit was during Sony years, which places it roughly 1995–1997, which is during Prince of Egypt's production period (released 1998). Paul was seeing this work in progress.

## The Iron Giant (Brad Bird, 1999)

**Hugely impactful.** Brad Bird is already on the map through The Incredibles and Ratatouille — now his earlier work lands with its own weight. The Iron Giant is a film about a weapon choosing not to be a weapon, about found family across radical difference, about institutional fear of the thing that isn't actually threatening. The [[protagonist-as-the-problem]] dynamic inverted — the Giant *could be* the problem, and chooses not to be. *"You are who you choose to be."* The [[role-authoring]] disposition expressed in a robot's self-determination.

## Saturday morning cartoons — Tom & Jerry, Warner Bros, Sid & Marty Krofft

Pre-conscious / self-choosing-childhood formation. Three distinct registers:

**Tom & Jerry** — pure physical comedy, timing as the entire art form, the [[protagonist-as-the-problem]] structure (Tom and Jerry are mutually each other's problem), no dialogue. The visual-timing tradition that runs through Tex Avery and Chuck Jones, absorbed in childhood before Paul knew what it was.

**Warner Bros. cartoons** — [[Chuck Jones]] territory; the full Looney Tunes register. Already substantially on the map; this is the childhood absorption layer that the Jones VTEL transmission built on.

**Sid & Marty Krofft** — the live-action / puppet fantasy shows (*HR Pufnstuf*, *Land of the Lost*, *The Bugaloos*) — a genuinely strange and psychedelic children's television register. The fae-and-the-weird in live-action form, before Paul knew what he was responding to.

## Hunger Games (books and films)

**Both excellent.** The dystopian-political register (Orwell/Huxley lineage) expressed in YA form, with sustained consequence (Katniss is genuinely damaged by what she goes through), the [[protagonist-as-the-problem]] dimension (the revolution and its costs), the female protagonist carrying the full weight of the narrative. The Capitol as the [[Chuck Jones|suits-in-the-room]] at civilizational scale.

# Animation films — specific influences

The Disney/Pixar/animation films Paul named specifically, as distinct from the general Disney/Pixar/Bluth traditions already on the map. The specificity of the list is itself information — these aren't "all Disney" but particular films in particular registers.

## The competence-and-right-context cluster

**Ratatouille** (Bird, 2007) — rat who belongs in a kitchen. The entire emotional core: being the thing you actually are in the context that lets you be it. "Anyone can cook" as thesis — not that everyone is equal, but that greatness can come from anywhere if the context is right.

**The Incredibles** (Bird, 2004) — superheroes who belong in action, forced into mundanity, eventually finding their right context. "When everyone's super, no one will be" — the tension between exceptional capability and institutional suppression of it. The [[role-authoring]] / [[Chuck Jones]] suits-in-the-room diagnosis expressed in animation.

**Zootopia** (Howard/Moore, 2016) — a rabbit who belongs on the police force, a fox who belongs as her partner, the city as a world of found cooperation across difference. The systemic-bias-as-antagonist register; the [[God Loves Man Kills]] / [[Chris Claremont|Claremont]] minorities-as-lens instinct in animated form.

All three are **films about finding your right context against institutional resistance** — which rhymes structurally with the [[role-authoring]] disposition, the Chuck Jones suits-in-the-room diagnosis, and the [[opportunity-orientation]] that watches for where the next real context is.

## The found-family-of-misfits cluster

**Robin Hood** (1973) — anthropomorphic animals, found-family-of-misfits adventure, the direct animation ancestor of *Bone* and the [[Frostfyre Faerie Glen|Jackalope Junction]] register. The lineage: Robin Hood → [[Jeff Smith|Bone]] → Jackalope Junction passes through this aesthetic.

**Lilo & Stitch** ([[Chris Sanders]], 2002) — Sanders is already on the map as a recurring-familiar figure; now his specific work lands. Found family across radical difference, Polynesian cultural specificity as authentic setting, the outsider who finds belonging. Same found-family pattern; different cultural register.

**The Princess and the Frog** (Clements/Musker, 2009) — New Orleans, jazz as [[music-as-worldbuilding-substrate]], Tiana as the build-it-yourself protagonist. The [[role-authoring]] instinct: she's going to open that restaurant through her own work, and the existing structures (race, class, magic) keep getting in the way.

**Mulan** (1998) — [[Chris Sanders]] was a story artist on the film and put himself in one of the scenes. Paul knows this. That's the [[visual-author-loyalty]] attention operating on animation: knowing the artist well enough to find them in the work, tracking the hand through the frames. Mulan also fits the competence-and-right-context cluster precisely — Fa Mulan is the quintessential *being the thing you actually are in a context that won't let you be it* story, with the institutional resistance explicitly gendered. Same emotional core as Ratatouille, Incredibles, Zootopia.

## The operatic-commitment cluster

**The Lion King** (1994, original) — the mythological scope, the Hamlet structure, the serious treatment of loss and consequence. Specifically the *original* — the [[visual-author-loyalty]] disposition operating on animation: the original artists' hand over the franchise continuation.

# Cross-stakeholder legibility

A function-tag and disposition surfaced explicitly through the [[9-language ML lipsync pipeline]] event, but applies retroactively to several pieces of Paul's work.

## Definition

**The disposition of treating *who can understand and use the system* as more important than *what the system technically does.*** Building technical work that legibly serves multiple professional traditions simultaneously, with translation built into the artifact itself rather than relying on intermediaries.

## The seam Paul lives on

Most engineers value technical capability over legibility. Most legibility-first designers lack the technical depth to build the underlying capability themselves. **Building both, with legibility prioritized, is a specific and unusual seam.**

This is the same seam [[Chris Sanders]] occupies as the only external figure Paul saw at *both* SDCC and SIGGRAPH the following week — *narrative-and-illustrative craft on one side, technical-graphics community on the other, walking the seam between them*. Paul's disposition is the same seam expressed in a different domain: *engineering-discipline on one side, multi-stakeholder usability on the other, walking the seam between them*.

## Where it shows up across the map

- **[[9-language ML lipsync pipeline]]** at Amazon — the prototype: ML-based facial animation usable by designers, producers, programmers, and artists
- **[[Building the Matrix]]** — strategic articulation of synthetic-data positioning at a level legible to founders, engineers, ML researchers, and potential commercial partners
- **[[pipeline_context.md]] / Sentient Machines pipeline architecture** — implementation that serves Harry's strategic understanding, the engineering team's implementation work, future ML researchers' consumption needs, and Paul's own architectural reasoning, in a single coherent artifact
- **The [[Frostfyre Faerie Glen]] booth** — at a different scale, the booth has to be legible to convention-goers from multiple aesthetic communities (fantasy fans, fairy-art collectors, jewelry buyers, casual passersby)
- **[[role-authoring]]** at the technical-output level — Paul's role descriptions, like his pipelines, are built to be legible across multiple audiences

## Why it's distinct from the [[dual-training-convergence]]

The dual-training-convergence (or technically the [[autodidact-foundational-stream]] + dual-training, after the Renderman/SIGGRAPH correction) explains *how Paul has the technical depth across multiple disciplines*. Cross-stakeholder legibility explains *what he does with that depth* — the choice to invest in making the technical work readable to non-specialists rather than letting depth be its own justification.

These are linked but distinct: the first is *capability*, the second is *disposition toward what to do with capability*.

## What this is *not*

- **Not** dumbing-down. Cross-stakeholder legibility doesn't mean simplifying to lowest-common-denominator; it means **building artifacts that operate at full technical sophistication while being readable from multiple vantages simultaneously**.
- **Not** documentation-as-afterthought. Most engineering teams treat documentation as the legibility layer. Paul's disposition is to bake legibility into the artifact's architecture itself, not to address it as a downstream task.
- **Not** evangelism. Paul isn't trying to convert audiences; he's trying to *serve* them — letting designers, producers, programmers, and artists each find what they need in the same system without being asked to translate.

## Connection to broader map

This concept rhymes with several already-named threads:

- The **[[fully-present-participant]]** disposition's *show up all the way to what's actually there* register — cross-stakeholder legibility is showing up to *what each audience actually needs*, not what's convenient to provide
- The **[[methodological-purist-anchor]]** demand for *examinability* — legibility is examinability extended to the audience layer, not just the technical layer
- The **anti-inflation register** Paul holds in his prose voice — Twain vernacular, Le Guin restraint, Theravada attention. Cross-stakeholder legibility is the same disposition expressed in technical-system architecture: *don't perform sophistication; build artifacts that earn their seriousness in service of who they need to serve*

For the upcoming Sentient Machines positioning conversation: **the cross-stakeholder-legibility disposition is part of what makes Paul's current work valuable to Sentient Machines specifically.** Harry needs the pipeline to be intelligible to him strategically; the engineering team needs it implementable; future researchers need it consumable. Paul has been building artifacts with this property for years. That's worth naming explicitly when the value of the current engagement is articulated.

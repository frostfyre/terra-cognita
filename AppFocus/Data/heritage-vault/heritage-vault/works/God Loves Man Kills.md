# God Loves, Man Kills

Marvel Graphic Novel, 1982. [[Chris Claremont]] and Brent Anderson. Self-reported as **highly influential**.

*The* Claremont X-Men thesis statement in concentrated form: the X-Men as persecuted minority, religious fundamentalism as antagonist, Magneto and Xavier in dialogue rather than combat, Kitty Pryde's "you mean kike" speech. A graphic novel that takes its political and ethical stakes seriously and credits the reader with the maturity to hold them.

**Function in the heritage map:** absorbing this as a teenager would have *reinforced what the [[comparative religion class]] was teaching academically* — that orthodoxies can be the problem, that institutional certainty is dangerous, that minorities-as-mutants is a workable lens for thinking about real social structures. Direct narrative reinforcement of the [[comparative-pluralist-method]].

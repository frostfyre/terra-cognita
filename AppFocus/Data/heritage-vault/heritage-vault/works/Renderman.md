# Renderman

Pixar's REYES-architecture renderer, the foundational production-quality offline renderer for film VFX from the late 80s through the 2000s. **Studied by Paul during the early Sony years, in parallel with the [[SIGGRAPH]] annual attendance window opening 1995.**

Studying Renderman in that era meant learning shading-language programming, displacement, custom surface shaders, ray-traced extensions — and crucially, **understanding the algorithms underneath the renderer**. It wasn't a checkbox tool; it was a programmable system you authored shaders into.

## How Paul actually accessed it

Without access to Pixar's proprietary tools at the studio, Paul's path into Renderman was via [[Blue Moon Render Tools]] — Larry Gritz's free Renderman-compliant renderer. That's the way the whole BMRT lineage worked: it implemented the Renderman Interface Specification with extensions Renderman didn't have at the time (ray tracing, radiosity, area lights), making the algorithmic foundations accessible to anyone willing to study them.

## Function in Paul's formation

The methodological-purist anchor's earliest expression. The choice to **study Renderman shaders rather than just use a renderer** is the original instance of the *understand-the-mechanism-underneath* disposition that runs through everything Paul has done since. Predates [[Blizzard library and academy]] by over a decade and [[Amazon Game Studios]]'s software-and-ML formation by closer to two.

Part of the [[autodidact-foundational-stream]] — self-directed training in algorithmic rendering and proceduralism, *while* doing PSX-era animation work professionally that didn't require any of it. Forward-investment self-directed study; paid off a decade-plus later when procedural pipelines and programmable shading became central to professional tech-art work.

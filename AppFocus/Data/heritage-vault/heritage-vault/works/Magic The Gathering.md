# Magic: The Gathering

Paul **learned and became obsessed with Magic** during his [[Sony San Diego]] years (mid-90s — Magic launched 1993). The right cohort and timing: Sony-launch-era animators getting into Magic in that window is a textbook entry into the game's first big audience expansion.

**Function on the heritage map: formative aesthetic education.** Magic was *the card game that taught a generation what fantasy art aesthetics were* — particularly for people in games industries who absorbed the cards as both a hobby and an aesthetic education. Magic single-handedly trained or sustained the careers of a substantial percentage of the contemporary fantasy painters who appear elsewhere in the map.

## The retroactively-discovered loop

**Paul did not know, when he later met [[Jeremy Cranford]] at [[Blizzard Entertainment]], that Jeremy had been an Art Director at [[Wizards of the Coast]] including on Magic during Paul's obsessive period.**

The art Paul was studying through gameplay obsession in the mid-90s at Sony was being commissioned by the man Paul would later co-curate the [[Blizzard library and academy]] with — and follow into [[FTX Good Luck Games]] — and remain friends with for decades.

That's a real loop, and it only became visible once this heritage map got built carefully. Function-tag: `retroactively-discovered-upstream-influence`. Different from instructor, colleague, or passive influence — Jeremy was shaping Paul's visual education *before the relationship existed*.

There's likely a sub-graph this loop opens up: **painters Jeremy commissioned for Magic that Paul absorbed before knowing Jeremy existed**. The map could excavate that thread by enumerating Magic artists from the relevant Cranford-AD era and tagging which ones already appear elsewhere in Paul's heritage. That's a real piece of ancestral graph work whenever there's appetite.

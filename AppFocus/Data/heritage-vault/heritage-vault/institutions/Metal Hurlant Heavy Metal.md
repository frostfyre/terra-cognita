# Métal Hurlant / Heavy Metal

**The institutional origin** of the European-comics tradition that runs through Paul's visual formation.

**Métal Hurlant** was the French comics magazine founded in 1974 by [[Moebius]] (Jean Giraud), Jean-Pierre Dionnet, Philippe Druillet, and Bernard Farkas. **Heavy Metal** was the American adaptation, launched 1977, which is the specific publication Paul absorbed during the bridge years.

## What the founding group established

The founding circle — Moebius, Druillet, Bilal, Caza, and others who appeared in its pages — collectively established a visual tradition characterized by:

- **Science-fiction-and-fantasy subject matter treated with literary seriousness**
- **Painterly rendering rather than line-only American comics conventions**
- **Page as designed surface** (inherited from [[Winsor McCay]], radicalized)
- **Willingness to be deliberately strange, slow, and non-narrative** in ways American mainstream comics weren't
- **European fine-art sensibility** brought into popular sequential form

## The American Heavy Metal

The American edition carried this tradition to a US audience that included Paul in his bridge years. The transmission: Métal Hurlant (Paris, 1974) → Heavy Metal (New York, 1977) → Paul reading in bridge years → the visual-language-of-the-page instinct that runs through his professional eye.

## Figures upstream and downstream

**Founding artists / core contributors:**
- [[Moebius]] — the most influential
- Philippe Druillet (baroque-cosmic, operatic)
- Enki Bilal (painterly, political, Eastern-European-inflected)
- Richard Corben (American, horror-fantasy, figure-obsessed)

**American Heavy Metal connection:**
- [[Simon Bisley]] — not a founding figure but appeared regularly; UK punk sensibility in the tradition
- [[Epic Illustrated]] — Marvel's attempt to import the same sensibility into American mainstream

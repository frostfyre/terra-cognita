# PowerAnimator and the SGI era

The platform context for Paul's [[Sony San Diego]] and early [[3DO]] work. **PowerAnimator** was Alias's flagship 3D animation package, the predecessor to Maya. Mid-90s production at Sony San Diego on PSX titles ran on PowerAnimator; the migration to early Maya (Alias|Wavefront released Maya 1.0 in 1998) was a major industry transition Paul lived through professionally.

The hardware: **Silicon Graphics workstations** — Indigo, Indy, Octane, Onyx — the only machines that could reasonably run real-time 3D in production at the scale games and VFX needed in that era. SGI dominated this seam from roughly 1992 through the early 2000s, after which commodity PCs caught up.

## Why this matters as a heritage entry

Two reasons:

**It's the platform context that made the [[autodidact-foundational-stream]] possible.** Studying [[Renderman]] via [[Blue Moon Render Tools]] required a workstation that could actually run those tools at usable speed. SGI hardware was that platform. Without the SGI workstation context, the algorithmic-rendering self-study would have been theoretical reading rather than hands-on practice.

**It established a professional-formation register Paul carried forward.** Working on $30k-$70k workstations running specialized OS and tools — IRIX, Open Inventor, the SGI ecosystem — meant being in a *specific kind of engineering culture* in the mid-90s. Tools were programmable. Source code was often available or licensed. Performance was a real constraint requiring architectural thought, not just optimization. The whole register of *technical artistry as engineering discipline* was native to this environment.

When [[Chris Adams]] later taught Paul Python / systems composition / design patterns at Amazon, he was building on a base that already understood what *thinking architecturally about technical-art tools* meant. The SGI-era formation is part of why Chris's teaching landed as fast as it did.

## The PowerAnimator-to-Maya transition

Specifically interesting because it's a transition Paul lived through *as a working professional*. PowerAnimator users had to relearn a substantially different tool when Maya arrived; the people who navigated that transition successfully were the ones whose underlying understanding of *why the tool did what it did* was strong enough to translate. Pure tool-fluency-without-understanding people often struggled with the migration. Paul navigated it; the [[autodidact-foundational-stream]] is part of why.

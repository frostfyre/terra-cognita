# Blizzard Entertainment

Seven years on **WoW**, [[Wrath of the Lich King|Lich King]] through [[Legion]]. The most consequential studio chapter of Paul's career.

Time-frame ~2008–2016 — the absolute peak of WoW as a cultural artifact and as an art-production organization. Lich King is widely regarded as the artistic and design high-water mark; Cataclysm was the great-world-revision experiment; MoP was the bold tonal pivot; WoD the troubled one; Legion the redemption arc and arguably the second peak. Paul worked through every major aesthetic and pipeline shift of WoW's mature era. **Not a job, a formation.**

Placement: **technical art department**, with significant time at the [[Blizzard library and academy]]. Paul was at the seam between art and engineering — the people who build the tools, write the shaders, author the rigs, set up procedural systems, make the impossible-for-artists possible and the tedious-for-artists automated. WoW tech art specifically: enormous content scale, aggressive performance constraints, art-direction-driven pipelines.

**The actual trade lineage of Paul's current work.** [[Sentient Machines pipeline|Sim2real synthetic data pipelines]] are a direct evolution of MMO-scale tech art instincts: still solving "how do we produce huge volumes of consistent, varied, controllable content with rigorous pipelines" — just for a different downstream consumer (a model instead of a player). The professional skeleton of what Paul does now was built in this department.

**Did not arrive here as a junior.** By the time Paul reached Blizzard he already had over a decade of shipped-title experience ([[Sony San Diego]] → [[3DO]] → [[VR1 Jaleco]] → [[Namco]] → [[Perpetual Entertainment]] → [[Secret Level SEGA]]). Blizzard wasn't where he became a games professional — it was a major chapter inside an already-established career. The seven years are at the *middle* of the arc, not the end.

Brought in by [[John Scharmen]] along with the rest of the [[Sony San Diego animation team]] — see Scharmen node for the cohort observation.

The chapter ends 2016, leading into [[Amazon Game Studios|Amazon]].

## How the chapter actually ended

Earlier draft framed the chapter ending as Legion concluding and Paul moving to Amazon following [[Joey Ray Hall]]. That's incomplete. **Paul was canned by an absentee boss who held the lead title while Paul ran the team in practice.** The boss ran different teams in name and was infuriated by Paul's deep engagement with the [[Blizzard library and academy]].

The cause Paul names: ***"He canned me when I stopped caring about unattainable carrots in career progression."*** Not a performance issue, not a culture mismatch — the predictable outcome of withdrawing from a coercion structure that was the absentee boss's only working tool. A boss whose authority depends on dangling carrots is structurally threatened when an employee stops chasing them, even if the work is good and the colleagues are well-regarded. Refusing the carrot is refusing the relationship.

This is consistent with the [[fully-present-participant]] disposition — *show up all the way to what matters; stop showing up to what doesn't*. Paul was fully present at the academy and in the actual team-running work, and stopped performing careerist desire toward an unattainable progression. The two are linked behaviors, not separate.

The Amazon transition therefore has both a *pull* ([[Joey Ray Hall]]) and a *push* (canned by the absentee boss). The [[high-trust-relationship-architecture]] pattern still holds — Paul moved through trust to Joey Ray Hall — but the chapter wasn't ended by his choice alone.

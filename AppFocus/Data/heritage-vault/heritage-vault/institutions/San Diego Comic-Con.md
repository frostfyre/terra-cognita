# San Diego Comic-Con (SDCC)

**Annual attendance 1995–2005** (at least), with an industry pass. Dropped off as the show became increasingly difficult to attend and increasingly oriented toward entertainment-industry promotion rather than comics and illustration.

## The arc of the show mirrors Paul's arc with it

- **1995 SDCC**: primarily a comics convention with growing film presence — the room Paul needed
- **Early 2000s**: the transformation begins, studio presence grows, Hall H becomes the promotional engine
- **2005 onward**: the entertainment-industry machine is dominant; the comics and illustration community is subsumed

Paul's dropping-off tracks **the moment the show stopped being the room he needed** — consistent with the [[SIGGRAPH disengagement]] pattern when that show stopped serving the classical-simulation community. Same mechanism: not hostility to the new direction, but accurate recognition that the room had changed and the value proposition no longer held. *Show up all the way to what's actually there; stop showing up when it isn't there anymore.*

## SDCC as the seam-walking convention

Paul attended both SDCC (narrative-illustrative-comics world) and [[SIGGRAPH]] (technical-graphics world) continuously through the same window. **That's the seam-walking disposition expressed in convention attendance.** Almost nobody maintains sustained presence in both rooms; Paul did for roughly a decade.

[[Chris Sanders]] is the only external figure Paul saw at *both* SDCC and SIGGRAPH the same week — which is remarkable precisely because Paul himself was one of the very few people bridging both rooms annually.

## The industry pass detail

Having an industry pass rather than attending as a member of the public is worth marking. It means Paul wasn't in the general-admission queue — he had access to the floor and to industry events on a professional basis. His SDCC attendance was *professional presence in the field's annual gathering*, not fan attendance.

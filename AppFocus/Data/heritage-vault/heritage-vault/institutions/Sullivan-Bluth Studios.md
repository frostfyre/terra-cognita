# Sullivan-Bluth Studios

Don Bluth's studio, operating out of **Dublin 1986–1995** — the Irish period. Founded with Gary Goldman and Morris Sullivan after Bluth's famous 1979 departure from Disney. Paul was a fan during the Dublin years.

**Key Dublin-period films:** *An American Tail* (1986), *The Land Before Time* (1988), *All Dogs Go to Heaven* (1989), *Rock-a-Doodle* (1991), *Thumbelina* (1994), *A Troll in Central Park* (1994), *The Pebble and the Penguin* (1995).

## What Sullivan-Bluth represents aesthetically

**American classical animation transplanted to Ireland** — the Disney-lineage tradition (full animation, weight and appeal, the *Bambi* / *Fantasia* sensibility) being produced on Irish soil during the same period Cartoon Saloon's founders were forming their own indigenous tradition. Different register from [[Cartoon Saloon]] — not drawing on Celtic mythology explicitly — but part of the same Irish animation context Paul absorbed.

Bluth explicitly positioned his studio as a recovery of Disney's pre-cost-cutting sensibility. That places Sullivan-Bluth in the **Disney classical tradition** running through [[Chuck Jones]] (different studio, same era and cultural conversation), [[Glenn Vilppu]] (Disney-tradition formation), and the broader classical-animation lineage Paul absorbed from multiple angles simultaneously.

## The Chuck Jones parallel

Both Jones (Warner Bros.) and Bluth (Disney exile) were reacting against institutional degradation of animation as an art form — Jones pointing at suits in the room ([[Warner Bros VTEL sessions]]), Bluth physically leaving Disney and building an alternative. Different responses to the same mechanism. Both represent **practitioner-led counter-institutional commitments to craft quality** in animation.

## The Sedona workshop

After the Dublin period, Sullivan-Bluth animators ran a workshop at the [[Sedona Arts Center]] — Paul and [[John Scharmen]] both attended. Details on timing and specific animators are not recalled. See [[Sullivan-Bluth Sedona workshop]] event node.

Note: the workshop was not in Phoenix during the studio's Phoenix period — it was a separate educational outreach event in Sedona, likely during Paul's post-Blizzard independent period.

## The two pillars of Irish animation Paul knew

- **Cartoon Saloon** — indigenous Irish visual tradition, illuminated manuscript aesthetics, Celtic mythology directly
- **Sullivan-Bluth** — American classical animation tradition, operating from Dublin, Disney-lineage sensibility

Together they represent what Irish animation meant in Paul's formation: both the native tradition and the transplanted tradition, encountered through the work and through the Phoenix workshop.

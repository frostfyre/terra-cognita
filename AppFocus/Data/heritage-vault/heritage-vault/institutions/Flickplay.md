# Flickplay

AR / Web3 / NFT social platform. Founded by **[[Pierina Merino]]** — the first female founder on Paul's heritage map. Low-risk crypto/NFT strategy (Paul's framing) with serious content processing requirements.

## The working situation Paul entered

Paul was trained by a fellow who was being managed out. That's a specific and textured professional context: the trainer had already lost institutional standing, which often produces a particular kind of candor — someone outside the political reward structure may share knowledge they'd otherwise hold closer, and may have a clearer-eyed view of what actually needs doing precisely because they're no longer managing toward institutional approval. **Knowledge from someone outside the political reward structure** is its own category of information.

## The content strategy transformation

**Before Paul arrived:** weeks-long process for users to receive their NFTs; business development involvement at each step; significant manual processing; expensive storage of 10,000+ element NFT collections; negotiation with collection creators required.

**After Paul built the system:** process-on-demand; users receive their NFTs within minutes; no collection-creator negotiation required; no expensive bulk storage of pre-generated collections.

This is a **fundamental architectural inversion** — not a marginal improvement. Instead of pre-generating and storing everything, generate on demand. Classic distributed-systems insight applied to a content-production problem. The improvement in user experience (minutes vs. weeks) and operational cost (on-demand vs. bulk storage) is substantial enough that it essentially transformed what the product was.

## "I never want to be the smartest person in the room"

Named at Flickplay as part of why the biz-dev side was never interesting and why looking for other work was the right move. **This is a real and important self-knowledge statement** and it connects directly to:

- [[assume-I-know-nothing]] — the epistemic stance that keeps investigation open; if you're the smartest person in the room, the learning slows
- [[eternal-student]] — always forming requires being in rooms where there's something to learn from others
- [[fully-present-participant]] refusal-edge — recognizing when a situation has nothing more to offer and moving

Not wanting to be the smartest person in the room isn't false modesty; it's **a practical commitment to the conditions that make continued formation possible.** When the room stops offering learning, move.

## Postscript: difficult to replace

After the pipeline was built and Paul began looking for other work, he did **some consulting to help find his own replacement.** The search proved difficult — turns out he is difficult to replace.

That's external confirmation of the [[role-authoring]] pattern from an unexpected angle. Not only does Paul write his own job descriptions, but when he leaves, the roles he constructs are hard to reconstruct from the outside. The difficulty of replacement is evidence of the specific combination at work: technical architecture *and* organizational context, [[cross-stakeholder-legibility]] built into the system rather than bolted on afterward. Most candidates have one; Paul had both.

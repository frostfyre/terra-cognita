# Society for Creative Anachronism (SCA)

Living-history organization doing medieval recreation — combat, costume, arts and crafts, heraldry, music, cooking, material culture. [[Chandra]] got Paul involved in the SCA during high school.

## What the SCA contributed

**Craft and making as serious practice.** Armor, fabric, calligraphy, illumination, leatherwork — these are taught and practiced with real rigor inside the SCA. This is formation in *making things well with your hands* that doesn't appear elsewhere in Paul's map. The [[Frostfyre Faerie Glen]] sculptures, crocheted figures, and jewelry have roots here — **the SCA is where the making-things register of Frostfyre was first formed.**

**Living-history worldbuilding through embodied practice.** Constructing a persona, researching the period, making the material culture, inhabiting the world. Collaborative worldbuilding — each participant contributing to a shared constructed reality. Adjacent to the visual worldbuilding Paul does professionally and to the [[Robert Heinlein|Heinlein]] competence-as-worldbuilding instinct: *demonstrate knowledge of how things actually work, in the medium of craft rather than prose.*

**Community of makers.** In the 1980s–90s the SCA was deeply intertwined with fantasy fandom, folk music, and the making culture that [[Frostfyre Faerie Glen]] exists inside. The conventions, the craftsmanship standards, the persona-building, the community warmth — all ancestral to the convention-booth culture Paul and Chandra operate in now.

## The Chandra origin

She got them involved. Same network-mover function Chandra plays repeatedly — seeing the community and bringing them both in. The SCA is therefore a Chandra-mediated formation, not an independently-sought one.

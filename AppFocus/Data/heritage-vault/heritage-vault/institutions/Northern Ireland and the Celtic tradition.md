# Northern Ireland and the Celtic tradition

**Direct physical presence** — Paul spent weeks in Ireland with **Derry as the home base**. Short time at each named location but real, lived experience: the Giant's Causeway, the Dark Hedges, Londonderry's walls and civic reality. Not ambient aesthetic absorption; the place was actually there, underfoot.

## Derry as home

Derry (Londonderry) as the base for Paul's Ireland weeks. The city: one of the most dramatically layered civic landscapes in the British Isles. The best-preserved city walls in Europe enclosing a living community rather than a heritage site. The Bogside murals visible from those walls — painted from outside by the community historically excluded from within. Post-Troubles recovery running alongside persistent deprivation. History that is not past.

**Paul's observation:** Derry reminds him of his hometown near [[Elmira NY and Twain|Elmira, NY]] — *"its sharp contrast between poverty and success, and the overcast weather."*

That's a somatic recognition, not an intellectual one. Paul recognized Derry because it felt like home. The poverty-and-success contrast unresolved and present in the landscape; the grey, heavy-skied weather; the sense of a place where multiple incompatible realities exist simultaneously without resolution. **That's the [[frisson-between-extremes]] aesthetic expressed in civic geography** — a place where the seam is visible and unsmoothed.

## The Giant's Causeway and the Dark Hedges

**Giant's Causeway** — ancient hexagonal basalt columns meeting the North Atlantic at the edge of Europe. Geological drama at the physical seam where land and sea meet at geological scale, with mythological narrative layered over it (Fionn mac Cumhaill). **Exactly the environment the [[frisson-between-extremes]] somatic disposition is drawn to** — two elemental domains meeting dramatically, the mythology acknowledging what the body already knows about the place.

**Dark Hedges** — the beech tree tunnel on the Bregagh Road, eerie and atmospheric. The canonical fae-landscape: a real physical space that looks like it belongs in a [[Charles De Lint]] or [[The Pinis|Pini]] world, where the mundane and the mythological are indistinguishable.

## The two pillars of Irish animation

**[[Cartoon Saloon]]** (Kilkenny, 1999–present) — the indigenous Irish visual tradition, descended from the illuminated manuscript and Celtic interlace aesthetic. *The Secret of Kells*, *Song of the Sea*, *Wolfwalkers*.

**[[Sullivan-Bluth Studios]]** (Dublin, 1986–1995) — American classical animation transplanted to Ireland. Don Bluth's studio producing *An American Tail*, *The Land Before Time*, *All Dogs Go to Heaven* from Dublin during this period. Different register from Cartoon Saloon — Disney-lineage animation being made on Irish soil — but part of the same Irish animation context Paul knew. Paul was a fan during their Irish days.

## The storytelling tradition

From the Ulster Cycle through [[WB Yeats]]'s Celtic revival to contemporary Irish animation. The deepest living well of the fae-in-the-natural-world storytelling tradition Paul has been absorbing since childhood — the pre-conscious mythology layer, the De Lint urban-mythic register, JB Monge, the whole contemporary-fae aesthetic all trace back to this tradition as a source.

Modern animation from this tradition: **[[Cartoon Saloon]]** (Kilkenny, Republic of Ireland) — *The Secret of Kells* (2009), *Song of the Sea* (2014), *Wolfwalkers* (2020). Confirmed. Their deliberately flat, decorative visual style descends from the illuminated manuscript tradition — the Book of Kells itself, Celtic interlace — expressed through animation. Same aesthetic root as [[Sergio Toppi]] in comics and [[JB Monge]] in painting. The contemporary animated expression of the same tradition feeding the whole contemporary-mythic cluster on the map.

## The weather register

Upstate New York and Northern Ireland share a meteorological aesthetic: grey, wet, heavy-skied. The kind of weather that makes interior light valuable and shapes a particular relationship to landscape. The aesthetic Paul works in at Frostfyre — De Lint, Pini, Monge, Wendling, the Northern-European-inflected fae tradition — is grey-lit, forest-and-rain-inflected. **The weather is part of the heritage, not incidental to it.**

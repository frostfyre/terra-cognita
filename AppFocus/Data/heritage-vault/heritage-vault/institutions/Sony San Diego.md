# Sony San Diego

Paul's first games industry chapter. PlayStation launch era — PSX launched September 1995. Paul's role: **animator** (taking lead before shipping). Not yet a "tech artist" — that title didn't exist in 1995 in games; Paul would import it from film at [[3DO]] around 1998.

Shipped: ***[[Spawn The Eternal]]*** on PSX (1997). Real production with a specific tech moment — early PSX 3D, pre-hardware-T&L, a generation defined by hand-rolled solutions to problems the hardware didn't solve for you. That era of game development *forged* a certain kind of technical artist: people who learned to make things work under brutal constraints, who think about memory and pipeline and budgets as second nature, who came up before the modern tooling smoothed everything over.

## The MoCap studio

**Paul was very involved with Sony San Diego's motion capture studio.** This is a real and significant professional detail that extends the physical/synthetic axis timeline substantially. The map's physical-capture work (Radiant Images 4D capture, Impossible volumetric pipeline) was understood as a post-Amazon addition to Paul's skillset — but the physical-capture-to-digital pipeline was part of his formation from the very beginning of his career, in the mid-1990s.

Mocap in 1995-97 was cutting-edge for game development. Sony San Diego having a facility and Paul being very involved means: exposure to the problems of translating physical human movement into digital character data at exactly the moment the technology was raw and required hand-solving. The same problems Paul later addressed through domain randomization and physics simulation in synthetic data pipelines — **how do you make captured physical reality function usefully as digital training data?** — appeared in prototype form at Sony through mocap.

**The GitS parallel** — Stand Alone Complex, which Paul watched later, used mocap data ("ghosts" in the data sense) to drive CG character puppets ("shells" in the animation sense). The production methodology accidentally mirrors the show's philosophical content: actual ghosts driving actual shells, in exactly the terms the GitS universe uses. Paul noticed this with amusement. The pattern-recognition that connects Shirow's 3D workflow to Paul's pipeline methodology was also operating here — the production of the show about synthetic consciousness used the same physical-to-digital capture process Paul had been working with at Sony.

**More than a job — this is where the cohort that would move forward across decades coalesced.** The [[Sony San Diego animation team]] was cohesive enough that members tracked each other across the following years. **Of the team, two people made the eventual transit to [[Blizzard Entertainment]] together: [[John Scharmen]] and [[Steve Aguilar]].** Earlier draft assumed a larger cohort wave; correction confirmed it was a tight pair. The rest of the team stays as Sony-era memory rather than as a Blizzard-era cohort.

Sony San Diego in the launch era was its own scene — Foster City and SD were Sony's American game development beachheads, and the people who came out of that period populated the entire western games industry afterward. There's likely a network of SCEA-era colleagues who matter in the map beyond Scharmen and Aguilar; status: open.

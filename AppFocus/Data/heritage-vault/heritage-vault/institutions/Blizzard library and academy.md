# Blizzard library and academy

Internal art library and the BlizzU / academy program at [[Blizzard Entertainment]]. A real institutional commitment — curated art books, life drawing, anatomy, color theory, master-study workshops, visiting-artist sessions. Most studios don't fund that seriously; Blizzard did, in that era.

**The genuinely unusual detail in Paul's seven years.** Choosing to spend a lot of time there *as a tech artist* (rather than just heads-down on tools and pipelines) tells the whole story:

Paul was *deliberately* keeping the painterly / illustrative / art-history side of his formation alive while doing technical work. **Not the default tech-art trajectory** — most tech artists drift toward pure engineering identity over time. Paul used Blizzard's resources to refuse that drift.

Probable consequences of that refusal:

- [[Frostfyre Faerie Glen]] exists as a serious creative venture rather than a hobby
- Professional positioning ("Building the Matrix," the LinkedIn craft) reads as *art-literate* in a way that distinguishes it from pure-engineer sim2real voices
- The [[Spectrum Fantastic road trip]] — a deliberate pilgrimage to the painters' convention — is consistent with someone whose eye Blizzard's library kept feeding for seven years

"Accidentally bumping into colleagues at workshops" is a heritage entry of its own — that's the *culture* as influence, not any single person. The fact that art workshops were dense enough with senior people that you'd run into them by accident says a lot about what Blizzard was at its best in that era.

## Named instructors (confirmed)

The academy wasn't just atmospheric — it was **taught**, by serious working figures, with real instruction. Paul had classes, painting sessions, and workshops with:

- **[[Tim Gula]]** — life drawing sessions. The foundational observational-discipline layer underneath everything else.
- **[[Steve Huston]]** — workshops. Bridgman / Boston-atelier figure mastery plus painterly stylization. The next layer up from raw observation.
- **[[James Gurney]]** — classes and painting sessions. The single most consequential instructor entry; observational imaginative-realist painting, methodological-purist exemplar in another medium.
- **[[Nathan Fowkes]]** — classes and painting sessions. DreamWorks-side color-and-design lineage, complementary to Gurney's imaginative-realist side.
- **[[Bob Watts]]** — classes and painting sessions. Founder of the Watts Atelier in San Diego (Reilly method); atelier-head-as-instructor.

Paul **still follows the published work** of those who publish. That's an active relationship to a tradition, not a memory of one — these instructors stayed in his live attention well past the formal sessions.

This is the *missing piece that explains why Paul's eye is what it is* — not just absorbed by proximity to [[Alex Horley]] / [[Wei Wang]] / [[Mark Gibbons]], but *actively trained* in the underlying disciplines of observational painting by serious working teachers, deliberately maintained for years. **The roster is structurally coherent** — foundations through figure mastery through color-and-light through atelier rigor — meaning whoever curated academy programming in Paul's era had taste and was building a real curriculum, not just hiring available painters.

## Curated by [[Jeremy Cranford]]

The institutional pattern observed above — *the roster is structurally coherent, atelier heads and figure masters and both major color-and-light lineages, deliberately curated rather than randomly available* — has a name: **Jeremy Cranford ran the academy programming.** The taste behind every painting-side instructor on this list, the workshops, the visiting-artist sessions, and the library curation was Jeremy's, brought from his prior chair as Art Director at [[Wizards of the Coast]].

**Paul was a regular, deeply engaged participant** — attended workshops, gave feedback, occasionally helped with cleanup and the practical running of the program. Not a co-curator (Jeremy was the curator); a fully-present participant who treated the program as his to extract maximum value from. That disposition itself is worth tagging — most colleagues would have attended some sessions and skimmed the library and called it good. Paul showed up all the way.

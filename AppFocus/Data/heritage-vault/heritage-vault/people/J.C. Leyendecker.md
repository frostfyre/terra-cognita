# J.C. Leyendecker

**Lineage node** — upstream of [[Nathan Fowkes]] and [[Glen Orbik]], and a key figure in the golden-age American illustration tradition.

Leyendecker (1874–1951) defined the visual language of early 20th century American commercial illustration — the Arrow Collar Man, over 300 *Saturday Evening Post* covers, and a figure-painting approach that combined **classical academic training** (he studied in Paris at the Académie Julian) with **graphic design sensibility** (simplified planes, strong silhouettes, deliberate color palette). His work sits at the seam between fine art and commercial illustration, demonstrating that the distinction was artificial.

His figure-painting method — constructing the figure from **tilted planes of color** rather than from line-and-fill — was deeply influential on later illustrators including Norman Rockwell (who explicitly cited Leyendecker as his primary influence) and through Rockwell on the entire subsequent American illustration tradition.

Fowkes cites Leyendecker directly. The plane-of-color approach to figure construction is visible in Fowkes's teaching about how to build forms with color shapes.

# Robert A. Heinlein

Bridge-era heavyweight set member alongside [[J.R.R. Tolkien]], [[Michael Moorcock]], and [[Frank Herbert]]. Function: *engineering-rationalism projected forward*. Hard SF in the competence-first tradition — characters who solve problems with knowledge and skill, libertarian-individualist political instincts, an underlying faith in *technical understanding as the path through any world*.

The trade-craft lesson at bridge age: **the protagonist's competence is itself the worldbuilding**. Demonstrating a character knowing how something works *is* the way the world becomes real. Opposite lesson from Tolkien (where the world is real because of accumulated invented history); complementary lesson to [[Frank Herbert]] (where the world is real because its systems interact).

**Function-tag of high importance:** Heinlein is the SF entry that connects most directly to Paul's professional disposition. The "Building the Matrix" framing of synthetic data, the intrinsic-annotation-as-differentiator argument, the LinkedIn craft of "Same word. Different discipline." — those are written in a *Heinleinian voice*, in the sense that the prose assumes the reader will respect competence and demand mechanism. Heinlein probably did more to shape **the voice Paul writes professional positioning in** than any of the fantasy authors. Apply this function tag to the prose-style axis.

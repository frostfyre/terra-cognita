# George Bridgman

**Foundational lineage node** for the structural figure-drawing tradition that runs through three of Paul's direct instructors: [[Steve Huston]], [[Bob Watts]] (via [[Frank Reilly method|Frank Reilly]]), and [[Glenn Vilppu]].

Bridgman (1865–1943) taught at the Art Students League in New York for decades. His approach: break the human figure into **geometric masses** — the thorax as a wedge, the pelvis as another, the limbs as cylinders and spheres — and understand how those masses *tilt and rotate relative to each other* before rendering the surface. His books (*Constructive Anatomy*, *The Book of a Hundred Hands*, etc.) were standard references for art students through the mid-20th century and remain in use.

## The lineage structure

Bridgman → **Frank Reilly** (systematized the head and figure approach) → [[Bob Watts]] / [[Watts Atelier]]

Bridgman → **Steve Huston** (who works in the Bridgman tradition with a more gestural application)

Bridgman → broader influence on **Art Students League culture** → [[Glenn Vilppu]] (who absorbed and modified it; gesture-first is partly a response *to* the Bridgman anatomy-first tendency)

## Why this node matters

Three of Paul's direct instructors trace back here. The pedagogical tradition Paul absorbed at the [[Blizzard library and academy]] (Huston) and at [[Sony San Diego]] and independently (Vilppu) converges on Bridgman as a shared upstream source. **Paul was absorbing variations of the Bridgman structural tradition from multiple angles simultaneously**, filtered through three different teachers with different emphases.

Vilppu's gesture-first approach is partly a *corrective* to the Bridgman anatomy-first tendency — Vilppu reorders the priorities (gesture before structure) without abandoning the underlying structural analysis. So Bridgman is both the tradition Vilppu builds on *and* the tendency he pushes back against.

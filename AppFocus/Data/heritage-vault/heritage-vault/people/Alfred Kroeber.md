# Alfred Kroeber

**Lineage node** — [[Ursula K Le Guin]]'s father. His importance to the map is entirely through her formation; he's not a direct influence on Paul but is the *upstream cause* of why Le Guin's worldbuilding has the anthropological character it has.

Alfred Kroeber (1876–1960) was one of the foundational figures of American cultural anthropology, a student of Franz Boas, and the leading anthropologist of California Native American cultures. He spent decades documenting languages, oral traditions, and material cultures that were disappearing, with an ethical commitment to accuracy and respect that was unusual for his era. His wife **Theodora Kroeber** later wrote *Ishi in Two Worlds* (1961), the biography of the last surviving member of the Yahi people — a book that was widely read and that Le Guin grew up with as part of her household's cultural air.

## What this means for Le Guin's formation

Le Guin grew up in a household where:
- Cultures are studied seriously, not exoticized
- Every social organization has its own internal logic worth understanding on its own terms
- The disappearance of ways of life is a tragedy worth documenting
- Fieldwork — going to the place, learning the language, living in the culture — is the right method

**Those instincts are exactly what make Le Guin's invented cultures feel real.** The Ekumen, the ansible, the ansible-connected societies of *The Left Hand of Darkness* and the Hainish cycle, the anarcho-communist society of *The Dispossessed* — all of them are built with the anthropologist's commitment to internal coherence and the ethnographer's attention to how a culture's material conditions shape its values.

Paul's Le Guin affinity, and the way the [[comparative-pluralist-method]] connects to her work, traces partly back to Alfred Kroeber's professional formation of his daughter. This is a lineage node worth having explicitly.

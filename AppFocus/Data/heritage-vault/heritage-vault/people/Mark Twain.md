# Mark Twain

The pre-conscious literature layer's most durable entry — handed to Paul as a child and never put down. Major works that stuck: *Tom Sawyer*, *Huckleberry Finn*, *A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur's Court*.

Twain's function in the heritage map is mostly *prose-level and dispositional*, not thematic. The vernacular American voice — sounds like a person talking, distrusts pretension, takes moral seriousness without ever announcing it — is probably doing more quiet work in Paul's sentence-level instincts than any of the fantasy writers later absorbed. Rhymes forward with [[Terry Pratchett]] (both moral satirists working in popular forms, both with the voice-of-a-real-person quality) and with the stated preference for compressed, reader-trusting prose ("Same word. Different discipline.").

*A Connecticut Yankee* is the proto-sim2real text in the early library: a modern technical mind dropped into a constructed historical world, building infrastructure, watching the system respond. Played for satire, but the structural concern is identical to what Paul works on now. Not a stretch — a genuinely unusual childhood favorite for someone who ends up building synthetic worlds for machines to learn from.

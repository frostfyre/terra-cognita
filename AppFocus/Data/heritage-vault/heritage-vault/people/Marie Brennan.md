# Marie Brennan

*The Memoirs of Lady Trent* series (A Natural History of Dragons and sequels). Victorian-naturalist-in-a-fantasy-world — the protagonist is a scholar and scientist rather than a warrior, and the heroic act is **research and documentation** rather than combat.

## The intellectual-as-adventurer register

Lady Trent is an adventure protagonist whose primary tool is systematic inquiry. She goes to the places, observes the things, documents them rigorously, and builds the knowledge base that eventually changes the world's understanding. The adventure is real; the stakes are real; the *method* is the protagonist's defining characteristic.

This connects to the [[methodological-purist-anchor]] disposition — the instinct toward examinability over approximation, understanding the mechanism underneath rather than accepting the approximation. Lady Trent is that instinct as a protagonist: she insists on actually knowing how dragons work rather than accepting the mythology.

The Victorian-naturalist framing also connects to the [[James Gurney]] influence — Gurney's *Dinotopia* is the same intellectual-adventure register, the naturalist-illustrator documenting a world with genuine scholarly attention. The naturalist-as-hero pattern appearing across Paul's influences in both prose and visual art.

# André Franquin

**Lineage node** — upstream of [[Claire Wendling]] and the franco-Belgian comics tradition that shaped her formation.

Franquin (1924–1997) — creator of *Gaston Lagaffe* and the definitive *Spirou et Fantasio* run, the most influential Belgian cartoonist after Hergé. His significance: he took the *ligne claire* (clear line) tradition and pushed it toward **expressive, gestural character drawing** — Gaston especially is a study in physical comedy expressed through body language, anticipation, reaction. The figures move.

He also created *Idées Noires* (Black Thoughts) late in his career — one page, one gag, pure dark satire, demonstrating that the same visual economy that works for physical comedy works for existential dread.

## What he passes to Wendling

Wendling's figure work — the emphasis on gesture, the economy of line, the sense that figures are *about to do something* — traces partly to Franquin's Belgian tradition. The French-Belgian comics school (Franquin, Hergé, Greg, Jijé) established that **line serves movement** rather than description, which is the tradition Wendling inherited and radicalized.

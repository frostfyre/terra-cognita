# John Lasseter

**Limited tier** — witnessed at the [[SDCC Miyazaki-Lasseter-Gaiman panel 2009]]; one of hundreds in the audience, not a personal encounter.

Paul: *"Lasseter's career story along with his work at Pixar (and his films of course)."* Two distinct objects of appreciation worth separating.

## Career story as [[role-authoring]] at institutional scale

The Lasseter career arc is one of the cleanest [[role-authoring]] stories in the history of animation:

- **Fired from Disney** in 1983 for advocating for computer animation — the suits-in-the-room mechanism [[Chuck Jones]] diagnosed, operating on him directly
- Joined Lucasfilm's computer division (later spun out as **Pixar**) — building the thing himself when the existing structures didn't serve
- *Luxo Jr.* (1986) and *Tin Toy* (1988) at [[SIGGRAPH]] — the early computer-animation shorts that demonstrated the medium had a future, presented to exactly the audience Paul was attending annually
- Negotiations with Disney that produced *Toy Story* (1995) — the film that changed animation permanently
- *A Bug's Life*, *Toy Story 2*, *Cars*, *Ratatouille* (as executive producer), *WALL-E*, *Up* — a production run of unusual consistency

**The structural move is identical to [[Joan Jett]] founding Blackheart Records after every major label rejected her, [[Mike Kennedy]] founding Magnetic Press, and Paul importing "technical artist" from film at [[3DO]].** Different scale, same pattern: *built the thing myself when the existing structures didn't serve.*

## Pixar as the organizational answer to the Jones diagnosis

[[Chuck Jones]] (at the [[Warner Bros VTEL sessions]]) diagnosed the mechanism of creative degradation: the suits in the room. Pixar under Lasseter and Ed Catmull was one of the few large-scale demonstrations that the alternative was possible — **an institution deliberately structured to keep artists in the room where creative decisions were made.** The *Braintrust* model, the story-first culture, the willingness to stop production and restart when the story wasn't working — all of these are institutional responses to the Jones mechanism.

Paul appreciating Lasseter's career story, with his own career structured around precisely the same problem (role-authoring, trust-network over institutional hierarchy, the Blizzard chapter ending over unattainable carrots), is the [[public-exemplar-of-stances-held-privately]] function at work: *this person enacted at scale something I've been enacting in my own register.*

## The full arc — misconduct context

The map's philosophy is accuracy over curation. In 2017 Lasseter took a leave of absence from Disney/Pixar following multiple accounts of workplace misconduct (unwanted touching, inappropriate behavior toward employees). He subsequently left Disney entirely and moved to Skydance Animation. The accounts were credible and multiple.

The map holds this as part of the arc rather than eliding it — the same way it holds Paul's Blizzard chapter ending honestly (fired, over unattainable carrots, not a voluntary transition). The career story Paul appreciates is the full one, including the complication. The role-authoring arc is real; the institutional failure in his own behavior is also real. Both belong on the map.

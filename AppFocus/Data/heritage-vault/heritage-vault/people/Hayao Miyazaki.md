# Hayao Miyazaki

**Limited tier** — Paul witnessed Miyazaki's only western public appearance at [[SDCC Miyazaki-Lasseter-Gaiman panel 2009|SDCC 2009]] (the *Ponyo* promotion panel with [[John Lasseter]] and [[Neil Gaiman]]). One of hundreds in the audience; not a personal encounter. Paul's framing: *"just one of the hundreds in the audience"* — accurate placement, real presence, not personal relationship.

Studio Ghibli director, animator, writer, and obsessive draughtsman. Paul specifies the influence: ***"especially his concept and storyboard art."*** That precision matters.

## Why the concept and storyboard work specifically

The films are widely loved. The *working drawings* are what Paul is naming as the specific influence. Miyazaki's concept art and storyboards (*ekonte*) are a different register from the finished films:

- The films are polished, color-complete, cinematically composed
- The storyboards are **loose, gestural, often expressionistic** in ways the finished film isn't — they show the *thinking* rather than the *result*
- The image boards (*image borudo*) that Miyazaki produces early in development — watercolor and pencil studies of mood, atmosphere, and narrative possibility before the story is set — are some of the most remarkable pre-production documents in animation history

**Why the drawings rather than the films:** The concept and storyboard work connects to the [[Glenn Vilppu|gesture-first]] disposition — the drawing as thinking rather than the drawing as finished product. It connects to the [[autodidact-foundational-stream]] register of *studying process rather than only product*. And it shows the [[frisson-between-extremes]] aesthetic in its *raw working form*, before the visual polish of the finished film normalizes it.

## The threshold-spaces obsession

Miyazaki is obsessively drawn to threshold-spaces — places where two worlds meet:

- The bath house in *Spirited Away* (human world / spirit world)
- The forest edge in *Princess Mononoke* (human / wild / divine)
- The flying machines in *Nausicaä*, *Laputa*, *Porco Rosso* (land / sky / between)
- The movement between spirit-world and physical-world in almost every film
- The childhood / adulthood threshold in *My Neighbor Totoro*, *Kiki's Delivery Service*, *Spirited Away*

**This is one of the most visually sophisticated treatments of the [[frisson-between-extremes]] aesthetic in animation.** The concept and storyboard art shows that threshold-obsession in its *raw working form* — the sketches of boundary-zones, liminal spaces, the edge where the known world gives way to the unknown. **If Paul is drawn to that work specifically, it's consistent with the seam-walking disposition in every other register of his life.**

## The Moebius connection

Miyazaki has cited Moebius as a direct influence — they corresponded, and their work shows mutual awareness. *Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind* (manga begun 1982) shows Moebius influence in its environmental-scale world design, its post-industrial decay, its visual treatment of technology as organic. Miyazaki is therefore partly a **downstream convergence of the [[Moebius]] influence** — someone who was already on Paul's map (absorbed through Heavy Metal in the bridge years) reappearing in a different medium with that lineage visible.

That makes Miyazaki a *bridge node* in the visual axis — connecting the European-comics tradition (via Moebius) to the Japanese-animation tradition (via Ghibli) via a practitioner who worked deliberately at that seam. **Seam-walking in the history of the medium itself.**

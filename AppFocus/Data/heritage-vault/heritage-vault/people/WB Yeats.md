# W.B. Yeats

**Ambient cultural context** — not a direct reading influence on Paul but the foundational upstream figure of the Celtic revival tradition that the contemporary-mythic cluster on this map descends from. Paul's framing: *"Yeats is ambient."* Accurate placement — he's in the cultural air that produced the works Paul absorbed directly, not a figure Paul has studied.

William Butler Yeats (1865–1939) — Irish poet, playwright, and central figure of the Celtic Revival. His collecting and elaborating of Irish mythology, faerie folklore, and occult symbolism in works like *The Celtic Twilight* (1893), *The Wind Among the Reeds* (1899), and his plays for the Abbey Theatre established the *literary and cultural infrastructure* that made the fae-in-the-modern-world register possible as a serious artistic mode in the English language.

## The lineage function

Without Yeats there's no Celtic literary revival; without the Celtic revival there's no tradition for De Lint to work inside; without De Lint there's no contemporary urban-mythic fae register; without that register, [[Frostfyre Faerie Glen]] has a different aesthetic context.

Yeats is therefore **three or four hops upstream of Paul's own work** — present as cultural substrate, not as direct influence. He belongs on the map as a lineage node for:

- [[Charles De Lint]] (urban mythic fae tradition)
- [[JB Monge]] (eerie-melancholy Celtic/folkloric otherworld)
- [[Northern Ireland and the Celtic tradition]] (the geographical-aesthetic node)
- The [[Frostfyre Faerie Glen]] contemporary-mythic register

## Connection to Northern Ireland

Yeats's work drew heavily on the mythology and landscape of the west of Ireland and the broader island tradition, including the Ulster cycle and the Fenian cycle that are specifically Northern Irish in geography. The [[Northern Ireland and the Celtic tradition]] node connects through Yeats to the broader Celtic revival literary tradition.

# David Tennant

Scottish actor. Paul's admiration extends across multiple roles — not just the Doctor but a broader performance range.

## The specific gift — holding incompatible registers simultaneously

The through-line across Paul's named admired roles: Tennant plays characters who hold registers that shouldn't coexist. **Warmth in the alien. Love in the demon. Good humor in the monster.** That's the [[frisson-between-extremes]] aesthetic expressed as performance rather than landscape or narrative. The same quality Paul responds to in Baker's Doctor, tracks in Sanders's films, sees in Harrison/Bugs Bunny. **The performer who makes the incompatible coexist.**

## The named roles

**The Tenth Doctor** (*Doctor Who*, 2005-2010) — the most emotionally expressive modern Doctor. The Doctor's loneliness and alien-ness communicated through performance rather than affect. "I don't want to go" as one of the more affecting regeneration moments in the show's history. See [[doctor-who-fourth-doctor]] for the formation context.

**Crowley** (*Good Omens*, 2019-2023) — a demon who has gone native on Earth and doesn't want to bring about the apocalypse. Tennant's Crowley loves humanity despite himself, expressed through the physicality (the car, the plants, the sunglasses doing more character work than most actors do with their faces). The role comes from the intersection of two already-established heritage nodes: **[[Terry Pratchett]] and [[Neil Gaiman]]** co-wrote the novel; Tennant embodying their collaborative character is a real convergence on the map.

Crowley is also an instance of the [[protagonist-as-the-problem]] pattern inverted — not a being whose defining trait destroys, but a being whose defining trait (demon, supposed to be evil) keeps producing the opposite of what it's supposed to. The demon who can't stop caring about humanity.

**Kilgrave** (*Jessica Jones*, Netflix 2015) — the villain. Absolute mind control; cheerful entitlement; the most genuinely disturbing antagonist performance in superhero television. Tennant plays Kilgrave with warmth and good humor, which makes him more disturbing than overt menace would. The show is explicitly about trauma and abuse; Kilgrave is a person who genuinely cannot recognize that other people have inner lives.

**This is the [[protagonist-as-the-problem]] pattern from the antagonist side** — and Tennant plays the cheerfulness *without* the self-awareness, which is the most chilling version. The monster who doesn't know he's a monster, played warmly. The incompatible registers held simultaneously in the most disturbing possible way.

## The Pratchett/Gaiman convergence in Good Omens

*Good Omens* is the specific intersection on the map where two independent heritage nodes meet in a performed work. Both Pratchett and Gaiman are named authors; their novel becoming a TV show with Tennant in it is the kind of convergence the map exists to surface. Worth noting also: the show's full-arc note for Gaiman applies here — the misconduct disclosures don't cancel the work, but the full picture is on the map.

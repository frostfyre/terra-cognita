# Daniela Perlein

**President of [[Sentient Machines]]**, with [[Harry OConnor]] as CEO. Has scaled companies before. **Does not need to do this job** — her presence is a deliberate bet on Harry and the company's thesis, not a career-ladder move.

## The weight of "does not need to do this job"

Someone who has scaled companies before and chooses to be President of a stealth-mode startup is making a loyalty-and-belief bet. Paul's observation: *"she's fond of Harry, and tries to make sure things are generally headed in the right direction."*

**"Fond of Harry"** is the tell. This isn't a mercenary executive relationship; it's a genuine belief in the person and the work. That's a specific kind of organizational investment that tends to be more durable than equity-only alignment.

**"Does not need to do this job"** is load-bearing in the same way the [[Charles Barnard|Barnard quote]] is load-bearing — it signals chosen presence rather than default. Companies that attract experienced executives who don't need the paycheck are different from companies that can only get people who need it. Daniela's presence is itself a signal about the quality of the organization Paul is working inside.

## What she actually does

**Instrumental in hiring and ops** — the two functions most critical for a scaling company. Hiring is where culture gets made or broken; ops is where vision meets execution reality. If Daniela owns those two functions well, Harry can stay in the technical work without the organizational machinery degrading around him. That's exactly the structure that makes a founder-led technical startup sustainable past the first few hires.

**"Tries to make sure things are generally headed in the right direction"** — presidential work, correctly described. Not micromanaging, not second-guessing the founder, but maintaining strategic vector and correcting when it drifts. The *generally* is doing real work — it's not *she ensures every decision is correct*, it's *she watches the overall direction and intervenes when needed*. Right role for someone with her profile alongside a technically-excellent young founder.

## She sees Paul whole

One detail that distinguishes this relationship from standard professional-colleague territory: **Daniela is excited about making prints from Paul's prop data.**

The sim2real pipeline Paul builds generates prop geometry — objects in scenes, physical assets for robotics training. That same geometry, from a different angle, is raw material for 3D prints. The same data file that trains a robotics model can produce a physical object that ends up in a [[Frostfyre Faerie Glen]] booth. **One file, two worlds — the [[seam-walking]] disposition expressed in the artifact itself.**

Daniela knowing about Frostfyre and being excited about the creative dimension of Paul's pipeline work means she sees Paul whole — not just the technical contractor, but the full range of what he does and makes. The people who see Paul's full range tend to be among the closest on the map: [[John Scharmen]], [[Jeremy Cranford]], [[Alex Horley]]. Daniela getting there relatively quickly in the relationship is notable.

It also makes the [[Sentient Machines]] chapter unusual in the map's history: **the professional and personal creative axes are in direct contact.** The prop data is the literal point where they meet. She has seen what it looks like when companies scale successfully and when they don't. She knows what strong technical contributors cost and what they're worth. She may have clearer vocabulary for Paul's value than Harry does, even if Harry has stronger intuitions about it.

The upcoming rate and scope conversation is therefore potentially legible to *two* different readers simultaneously — Harry's technical-founder read and Daniela's experienced-executive read. The [[cross-stakeholder-legibility]] disposition Paul has been building for decades is directly relevant: make the value clear to both the technical reader and the operational reader in the same articulation.

**The Barnard frame applies here too**: *"I don't know what he does, but things are better with Paul on the team."* Daniela, with her scaling experience, probably has more specific vocabulary for this sense than Harry does. She's seen enough companies to recognize what the Paul-shaped contribution is worth.

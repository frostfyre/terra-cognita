# Charles De Lint

Bridge-era literature anchor. Urban / mythic fantasy, place-as-character, fae-in-the-modern-world rather than high-Tolkien. Together with [[The Pinis]] and the illustrative tradition (Brian Froud, Arthur Rackham, Charles Vess) defines the *contemporary mythic* register that the [[Frostfyre Faerie Glen]] venture works inside.

Functionally adjacent to [[JB Monge]] on the visual side — the eerie-melancholy Celtic/folkloric otherworld is the same sensibility expressed in different media.

## Music-as-worldbuilding-substrate

Per Paul's later observation: De Lint **uses music as a deliberate compositional convention** — Celtic, folk, and indie traditions referenced explicitly, musicians named, Newford novels structured around shows and recordings. He talks about this method when discussing his work. **Music is load-bearing diegetic texture, not decoration.**

This places De Lint in a structural pair with [[Bryan Lee O'Malley]] across generations and surface registers — same convention, different genre. See [[music-as-worldbuilding-substrate]] for the full reading. The instinct connects to [[Frank Herbert]]'s use of ecology as substrate in *Dune* — *coupling the imagined world to a real one with serious texture*.

That convention shows up in Paul's life directly: [[Scott Pilgrim]] (which executes it in indie-rock register) demonstrably introduced him to [[Metric]]. The De Lint version of the convention probably did similar work earlier; status: open.

# Frank Frazetta

**Major lineage hub** for the American heroic-fantasy painting tradition on Paul's map. Upstream of [[Brom]], [[Alex Horley]], [[Simon Bisley]] (filtered through British-punk sensibility), and much of the Blizzard house aesthetic. Also downstream of [[Howard Pyle and the Brandywine school]] — Frazetta absorbed Pyle and NC Wyeth's dynamism and transformed it into something more muscular, kinetic, and darkly erotic.

## Formation

Studied as a teenager with cartoonist John Giunta; did newspaper comics (*Johnny Comet*, ghost-work on *Li'l Abner*) in the 1940s–50s before moving into fantasy and science fiction illustration for Ace paperbacks and *The Edgar Rice Burroughs* series. The transition from comics to oil-paint fantasy illustration in the 1960s–70s is where his distinctive style crystallized.

## What Frazetta established

The **American heroic-fantasy painting tradition** as a coherent aesthetic with specific features:

- Dynamic, low-angle composition that makes figures loom and dominate
- Deep, saturated color in the shadows; high-key highlights on flesh and metal
- Muscular idealization that descends from classical sculpture through NC Wyeth
- Narrative compression — the entire drama of a story in a single image
- The forest / clearing / ruins as default environmental register (influenced by NC Wyeth's Brandywine-school outdoor settings)

Almost every working American fantasy painter in the second half of the 20th century was working either *with* or *against* this template. Brom absorbed and darkened it; Horley absorbed and Europeanized it; the Blizzard house style absorbed and gamified it (more readable silhouettes, higher saturation, cleaner reads at smaller size).

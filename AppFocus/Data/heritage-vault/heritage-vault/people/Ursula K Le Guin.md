# Ursula K. Le Guin

Bridge-era anchor on the literature axis. Function: *anthropological worldbuilder*, prose discipline, ethical seriousness. Where [[Michael Moorcock]] is operatic and [[J.R.R. Tolkien]] is mythopoeic, Le Guin treats invented cultures as objects of study — secondary worlds as ethnography.

Lines up unusually well with the religion-of-the-week / comparative-religion-class layer of Paul's formation. The disposition of "multiple coherent ways to organize reality, each illuminating differently, none absolute" is *Le Guin's working method* as much as it's a personal stance. Reading her at the bridge age would have reinforced what was already being practiced informally at home and would later be formalized in coursework.

Counterweight to the operatic-romantic register of Moorcock — the prose is restrained, the politics serious, the magic systems treated as systems of signification rather than power. Adds a register Paul has access to but uses sparingly: the disciplined-quiet voice.

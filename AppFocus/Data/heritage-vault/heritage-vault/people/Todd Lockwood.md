# Todd Lockwood

Fantasy illustrator and writer. Paul reads both his art and his writing — the [[visual-author-loyalty]] pattern in its purest current form: **following an artist into their prose because you trust the hand.**

## The illustration work

Lockwood is a major figure in classical fantasy illustration — his dragon work defined the visual language of Dungeons & Dragons 3rd edition (late 1990s–early 2000s) and he has worked extensively in the fantasy publishing cover art space. His tradition: technically rigorous, Brandywine-lineage draftsmanship applied to dragon and fantasy subjects, the [[James Gurney]] / [[Donato Giancola]] / [[Howard Pyle and the Brandywine school|Brandywine]] tradition in contemporary fantasy illustration form.

His dragons specifically are notable for being *anatomically considered* — he approaches them as a naturalist would, thinking through musculature, flight mechanics, scale function. The same naturalist-documenter instinct as [[Marie Brennan]]'s Lady Trent, expressed in illustration form. Lockwood and Brennan are therefore doing adjacent things in different media.

## The writing work

*The Summer Dragon* (2016) and sequels — the same dragon-focused world expressed in prose. Paul follows Lockwood into the prose. That's a real statement of trust: the visual vocabulary has earned enough credibility that Paul wants to see how the same mind constructs a narrative.

Worth marking as the **visual-author-loyalty function extended to prose**: not just following Wendy Pini's line through Elfquest issues, but following Lockwood from illustration into fiction because the sensibility is trusted across media.

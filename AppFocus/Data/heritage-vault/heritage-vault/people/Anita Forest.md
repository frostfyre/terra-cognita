# Anita Forest

Paul's paternal grandmother. Wife of [[JG Forest]]. Lived down the street through Paul's formative years; Sunday dinners a weekly tradition.

**The primary culinary formation figure on the map.** French Canadian cooking transmitted directly — person to person, kitchen to kitchen, across the Sunday dinners that were a fixture of Paul's childhood. Not a cookbook influence; not a celebrity chef absorbed through television. The oldest and most complete form of food education: watching, helping, learning by doing, in someone's actual kitchen.

## French Canadian culinary tradition

French Canadian cuisine: rooted in the same culinary grammar as classical French cooking but filtered through Quebec's specific geography and history. Tourtière, pea soup, the preservation traditions of a northern agricultural culture, the Catholic feast-day cooking calendar, the practical richness of food built for cold winters and large families.

This tradition connects to the [[family-formation-context|Catholic calendar formation]] — feast days have specific foods; the liturgical year and the culinary year are the same year. Anita's cooking was probably structured around that rhythm. Paul absorbed it as a child without necessarily knowing that's what he was learning.

## The Sunday dinner ritual

Weekly gathering, grandmother cooking, the table as the center of family life. Combined with the Walt Disney Sunday nights and the ritual-formation structure of Paul's childhood, **Sunday was the day of gathering and repetition** — the day defined by return to the same people and the same practices. The food at those tables is part of the formation in the same way the films and the conversations were.

## JG's Manhattans

[[JG Forest]] drank Manhattans. Paul has carried this with him. The specific drink as a carried tradition — not just "I drink Manhattans" but "I carry this from my grandfather." **A memorial practice**: the drink that keeps a person present. In the same register as the annual Lion in Winter viewing or the Rarebit Fiend sought repeatedly — a thing you return to because it connects you to something real.

The Manhattan: rye or bourbon, sweet vermouth, Angostura bitters, cherry. A serious and elegant drink with a specific grammar. That JG drank them and Paul carries the tradition is the most intimate kind of inheritance — a taste memory that lives in the body.

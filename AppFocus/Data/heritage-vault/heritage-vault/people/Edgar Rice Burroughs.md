# Edgar Rice Burroughs

Read Tarzan and John Carter. **The personal story is more of an influence than the books themselves** — a precise and honest self-observation worth marking on the map. The work provided the reading; the formation story provides the lasting influence.

## The ERB formation story

Burroughs was forty years old, a commercial failure across multiple ventures (including pencil sharpener salesman, gold miner, railroad policeman), when he started writing fiction. His reasoning, reportedly: if the stuff being published in pulp magazines was that bad, he could do it too. He was essentially right — his first Tarzan story (*All-Story* magazine, 1912) and his first John Carter story (*Under the Moons of Mars*, 1912) invented two of the 20th century's most durable characters in his first attempts at fiction.

**Late start. Multiple prior failures. No writing background. No institutional support. Built the thing because he figured he could.** That's the most extreme version of the `late-start-against-the-odds-formation-as-inspiration` pattern on the map — more extreme than Rowling (who was young), more extreme than Joan Jett (who had musical background). Burroughs was a middle-aged failed businessman who invented Tarzan.

## Why the story over the books

The books are adventure pulp — genuinely imaginative (especially the John Carter Barsoom worldbuilding) but not Paul's primary aesthetic register. The *story of how ERB became ERB* is the influence: proof that the late start is possible, that the multiple-failure arc doesn't foreclose the real thing, that you can build from nothing without prior credentials in the specific domain.

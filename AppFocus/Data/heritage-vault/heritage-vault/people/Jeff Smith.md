# Jeff Smith

**Limited tier** — multiple occasions at [[San Diego Comic-Con|SDCC]] across Paul's annual-attendance decade (1995–2005), with brief 1:1 conversations. Also a recent library tour appearance. Real direct contact, not personal relationship.

Creator of ***Bone*** (1991–2004) — one of the great independent comics achievements of the 20th century. Self-published through Cartoon Books for its entire original run.

## The most direct creative-lineage link on the heritage map

Paul: *"much of Jackalope Junction has inspirations from Bone."*

**This is the first place on the entire map where a named external figure's specific work has a documented, direct influence on Paul's own created work.** Most heritage entries are influences on taste, professional practice, or worldview. Jeff Smith → *Bone* → [[Frostfyre Faerie Glen|Jackalope Junction]] is **influence-on-artifact** — the thing Paul made carries Smith's work in it. That's a different and stronger relationship than anything else on the visual axis.

## What Bone contributes to Jackalope Junction

*Bone* is **found-family-of-misfits adventure-comedy with deep mythological stakes underneath a deceptively simple cartooning surface.** The Bone cousins (Fone, Phoney, Smiley) are absurdist cartoon figures dropped into a fully-realized epic-fantasy world with serious internal mythology (the Dreaming, the Lord of Locusts, the ancient history of the Valley). The humor and the mythological weight coexist without canceling each other.

Jackalope Junction, with its fae-adjacent anthropomorphic-animal characters, is working in this same register — the **accessible surface that carries real mythological weight**, the comedy-and-seriousness coexistence, the found-family structure in a world with bigger stakes underneath.

This adds a dimension to Jackalope Junction's heritage that the [[Charles De Lint]] / [[The Pinis]] axis alone doesn't capture. De Lint and the Pinis are earnest-and-operatic; Smith is accessible-and-comic with depth emerging gradually. **Jackalope Junction probably draws on both registers simultaneously.**

## Smith's own lineage — the McCay connection

Smith has explicitly cited **[[Winsor McCay]]** (*Little Nemo*), **[[Walt Kelly and Pogo|Walt Kelly]]** (*Pogo*), **Carl Barks** (*Uncle Scrooge*), and **Charles Schulz** (*Peanuts*) as primary influences. 

**That means [[Winsor McCay]] connects to Paul's map through two separate channels:**
1. Via [[Moebius]] (European formal-invention tradition)
2. Via Smith (American cartooning tradition)

McCay is therefore a more structurally central node than his original stub placement suggested — a real bridge between the European and American sequential-art traditions, running through different downstream channels.

## The self-publishing model

Smith self-published *Bone* through Cartoon Books for its entire original run before eventually licensing to Scholastic for the color editions. That's another instance of the **[[role-authoring]] at publishing scale** pattern — built the thing himself when the existing structures weren't serving the work. Same family as [[Joan Jett]], [[Mike Kennedy]], [[Taylor Swift]].

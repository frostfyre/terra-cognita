# Chris Sanders

Brief encounters many times over the years at [[San Diego Comic-Con|SDCC]] and [[Lightbox Expo|LBX]]. Its own category — the *recurring familiar* — neither one-shot nor sustained.

Disney Renaissance animator (*Lion King*, *Mulan*, *Beauty and the Beast*), creator of *Lilo & Stitch*, director of *How to Train Your Dragon* and *The Wild Robot*. **Currently at DreamWorks.** Draughtsman of distinctive, economical, character-first line work.

**Sanders was a story artist on *Mulan* and put himself in one of the scenes.** Paul knows this — the [[visual-author-loyalty]] attention operating on animation: tracking the artist through the work, knowing the hand well enough to find it in the frames. Sensibility is the most *animation-pure* one in the visual roster — slightly different lineage from the painters.

## The function on the map (corrected)

Earlier framing centered the studio credits. **The actual function for Paul is the personal-creative-practice maintained alongside the major-studio career.** Sanders' independent illustration work (the books, the personal pieces, the public sketching) running parallel to his studio output is the disposition Paul finds inspiring — Paul: *"that he still does creative work apart from the big companies inspires me."*

That places Sanders structurally alongside [[Brom]] as a [[public-exemplar-of-stances-held-privately]] entry: the visible model of *maintaining a distinct creative voice through personal work even while operating inside major-studio careers*. Brom does this in the more-independent register (running his own IP, painting his own books). Sanders does it in the more-integrated register (still inside studios but with a clearly identifiable personal practice). Same function expressed at different points on the spectrum.

**Direct parallel to Paul's own [[Frostfyre Faerie Glen]] practice** — the personal-creative-venture maintained alongside the technical-art career. Sanders is the public exemplar of the disposition Paul has been enacting privately for years.

## The crucial fact for the heritage map

Sanders is the **only person outside Paul's immediate circle** that Paul saw at *both* SDCC and [[SIGGRAPH]] the following week. SDCC is the comics/film/pop-culture room; SIGGRAPH is the technical computer graphics room. Almost no one bridges those personally — they're different tribes with different conventions, different talks, different week. The kind of artist who shows up at both is unusual: someone whose practice sits at the seam of *narrative-illustrative craft* and *technical-graphics community*.

**That's basically the same seam Paul lives on.** Sanders being the one external recurring figure across both rooms isn't coincidence — he's the public-facing exemplar of the disposition Paul actually shares. Function-tag: `seam-walker-between-narrative-and-technical-graphics`. Apply this to Paul as a self-tag too — the [[seam-walking]] meta-concept now formalizes this.

## The Croods and the Betterman parable

**The Croods** (2013, Sanders co-directing with Kirk DeMicco) — prehistoric family forced from their cave by geological cataclysm; they meet Guy, an inventor who represents curiosity and adaptation rather than fear-based survival. The Croods' rule: *"Never not be afraid."* The film's argument: the rule kept them alive but isn't sufficient for what's coming.

**The Croods: A New Age** (2020) — introduces the **Bettermans**, whose surname is the joke. Phil Betterman is a tech-bro type: he's built an elaborate compound with optimized systems, curated garden, labor infrastructure (the "punch monkey" system). Everything is controlled, efficient, and separated from the messy reality of wild nature.

**Paul reads this as a tech-bro parable** — and it is. The Betterman critique is specific:

- Phil has **solved all the proximate problems** through technological optimization but created new dependencies and brittleness underneath
- His systems are efficient but **fragile** — they don't account for actual complexity; they exclude it
- The **name is the tell**: the conviction that you're better, that your optimization is superior, is itself the failure mode
- The Croods' apparently dumb **direct engagement with the world** is more resilient than Phil's curated control of it
- The film argues that **technological sophistication without integration into genuine relationship** is a form of impoverishment that looks like success

**Why this lands for Paul specifically:**

The Betterman failure mode is the opposite of what Paul builds. The [[Sentient Machines]] synthetic data pipeline is not about naive optimization — it's about understanding the actual problem (what does a robot need to learn to function in reality?) deeply enough to build simulation that captures the genuine complexity rather than curating it away. Domain randomization is the explicit acknowledgment that reality is messier than any curated dataset. **Phil Betterman would ship a pristine curated dataset; Paul's pipeline ships the chaos.**

The [[assume-I-know-nothing]] and [[eternal-student]] dispositions are the anti-Betterman stance — the person who believes they've figured it out has stopped learning. The person in Paul's preferred rooms (where he's *not* the smartest person) is practicing the opposite of Bettermanism.

This connects directly to the [[Chuck Jones]] suits-in-the-room diagnosis: the suits don't make cartoons bad through malice but through the certainty that they know better. **Bettermanism is the cognitive structure that produces the suits.** Sanders making this critique in a family animated film — accessibly, without winking — is the [[commitment-to-the-bit]] disposition applied to social commentary.

## Mid-to-current career work

**How to Train Your Dragon** (2010) — Sanders directed alongside Dean DeBlois. The Hiccup/Toothless found-family-across-radical-difference relationship; the outsider-who-finds-his-right-context structure; flight sequences as [[frisson-between-extremes]] somatic experiences done right. The series expanded across three films (2010, 2014, 2019) — a long-arc animated world built over a decade. The sequel *How to Train Your Dragon 2* (2014) is widely considered one of the best animated sequels made, darkening the emotional register without betraying the first film's tone.

**The Wild Robot** (2024) — Sanders's most recent and arguably most mature film. Roz the robot washes ashore on a wild island, learns to survive, adopts an orphaned gosling.

Paul's assessment: *'Wild Robot has some amazing thematic work under all the amazing animation work.'* That distinction matters — the animation is extraordinary on its surface, but Paul specifically names the thematic depth underneath as a separate achievement. The film asks **what makes a mechanical being genuinely alive** — the same question as *Ghost in the Shell*, expressed in a family-film register without condescending to it. The [[commitment-to-the-bit]] recognition operating: seeing that the film is doing more than its technical achievement suggests. Strong [[operatic-tragic-register]] notes in the third act that the surface register earns rather than apologizes for.

*'Post-Disney work is some of his best, especially The Croods.'* The post-Disney arc — Croods (2013), HTTYD series, Wild Robot — represents Sanders with full creative authority. The Betterman storyline *'particularly good'* — the thematic critique operating cleanly without studio machinery that might have softened it.

**Personal illustration work** — Sanders maintains an active personal illustration practice alongside the studio work: books of personal art (*Kiskaloo*), convention sketches, the specific hand that Paul has been following across SDCC and Lightbox appearances over the years. This is the [[public-exemplar-of-stances-held-privately]] function in its most specific form: Paul can see the personal practice in the work, track the hand, find Sanders in the frames.

[[Lightbox Expo]] is one of the recurring contexts where Paul has encountered Sanders. Worth flagging because Lightbox is itself a substantial bridge institution on the map (founded by [[Bobby Chiu]]), connecting many of the named figures in the contemporary-illustration cluster.

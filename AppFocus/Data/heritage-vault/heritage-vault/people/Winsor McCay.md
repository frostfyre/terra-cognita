# Winsor McCay

A more structurally central node than initial stub placement suggested. McCay connects to Paul's map through **two separate channels** and Paul has *actively sought out* a specific body of McCay's work on multiple occasions — which is a stronger relationship than the passive-influence tier usually implies.

## The dual channel structure

**Channel 1 — European formal invention:** McCay → [[Moebius]] (formal invention on the page, French bande dessinée tradition, the treatment of the page as a designed surface)

**Channel 2 — American cartooning tradition:** McCay → [[Walt Kelly and Pogo|Walt Kelly]] → [[Jeff Smith]] → *Bone* → [[Frostfyre Faerie Glen|Jackalope Junction]]

That makes McCay a **bridge node between the European and American sequential-art traditions** on Paul's map — the single figure whose influence runs through both lineages. In Gephi this will show up as unusually high betweenness centrality relative to his apparent simplicity as a historical figure.

## *Little Nemo in Slumberland* (1905–1914)

The famous work — formally inventive, visually elaborate, treating the Sunday comics page as a site of architectural and compositional experimentation. Panels expand, contract, tilt, shatter; the page as a whole is a designed surface. This is the formal-invention register that connects to Moebius.

## *Dream of the Rarebit Fiend* (1904–1913)

Paul has **sought this out on multiple occasions** — a specific and active pursuit rather than passive absorption. The distinction matters on the map.

*Rarebit Fiend* is McCay's other major strip, less famous than *Little Nemo*, **darker and stranger**. The gimmick: every strip ends with the character waking from a nightmare induced by eating Welsh rarebit before bed. The content of the nightmares is extraordinary: body horror, existential dread, surreal transformation, scale distortion, physics violation, loss of control over one's own body and identity. McCay was exploring psychological and unconscious imagery in 1904 using sequential art — before Freud's work was widely known in America, before surrealism existed as a movement.

**Why Paul has sought it out specifically and repeatedly:**

The register it operates in — psychological transformation, surreal body-horror, nightmare logic as visual structure, the seam between waking and dreaming — is **the deepest expression of the [[frisson-between-extremes]] aesthetic** on the map. *Rarebit Fiend* explores the threshold between rational daily existence and nightmare transformation, between the known self and a horrifying version of it, between physical stability and grotesque dissolution. **That's the consciousness-level version of the seam-walking disposition** — not landscape boundaries or disciplinary seams but the seam within experience itself, between waking and dreaming, between self and its dissolution.

Seeking it out repeatedly means Paul returns to it as a reference point — a *Rarebit Fiend* function on the map: a work Paul actively pursues because the register it operates in is one he's drawn to and can't find elsewhere in quite the same form.

## Formation

McCay (1866–1934) was self-taught as a draftsman, absorbed influences from chromolithography and circus art as a young commercial artist, then developed his distinctive architectural-perspective-and-fantasy approach through the newspaper strip format. The formal invention is partly derived from the technical demands of newspaper printing — he learned to use the Sunday color-supplement page as a total designed surface.

## The animation connection

*Gertie the Dinosaur* (1914) was one of the first theatrical animated films — McCay was doing character-with-personality animation before there was an animation industry. That connects him to [[Chuck Jones]] and [[Glenn Vilppu]] through the broader animation lineage, though the connection is historical rather than direct.

# Michael Moorcock

**The beginning of Paul's "collection."** Pre-teen entry, the moment reading shifted from *finding things to read* to *building a library*. Self-conscious move that prefigures the same disposition manifesting in the visual / professional library at [[Sony San Diego]] roughly fifteen years later.

Center of gravity: **Elric**. The anti-Conan, anti-Aragorn — doomed albino sorcerer-emperor with the soul-drinking sword, fighting his civilization's decadence and his own physical frailty, locked into a cosmic cycle he can't escape. Reading Elric at the pre-teen age means absorbing *cosmology and operatic register* before the literary sophistication exists to filter them. They land in the bone, not the catalogue.

What Moorcock seeded:

- **Law / Chaos / Balance** as cosmology — both extremes pathological, balance the only sustainable position. Underlies AD&D alignment, Warhammer's metaphysics, much of British fantasy. The metaphysical instinct in Paul's fantasy reading is *balance/tension/cycle*, not good/evil/redemption — that's a Moorcock inheritance, reinforced by [[Theravada Buddhism]] via mother and the [[comparative religion class]].
- **The Eternal Champion** — multiple instances of the same archetype across realities, connected, recurring, each working through their version of the cycle. **Structurally a procedural idea.** Same intuition as parametric character generation, instance-with-variation, the same skeleton expressed across different domain randomizations. Function-tag: `worldbuilding-mode-recursive-multiverse` — direct ancestor of the [[domain randomization]] disposition.
- **Protagonist-as-problem.** Elric is closer to a Shakespearean tragic figure than a fantasy hero. Reading him as a kid teaches that protagonists can be *the problem*, not the solution. Reinforced later by [[Dark Phoenix Saga]] (operatic tragedy, godlike power, self-destruction to protect those loved) — same structural pattern in superhero mode.
- **Operatic-romantic prose register** — fast, lush, unembarrassed by melodrama. Counterweight to [[Ursula K Le Guin]]'s restraint and [[Mark Twain]]'s vernacular. Paul has both registers available; Moorcock is the operatic pole.

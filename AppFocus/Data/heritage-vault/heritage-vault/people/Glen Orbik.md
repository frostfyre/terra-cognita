# Glen Orbik

**The person who introduced Paul to [[Tim Gula]]** — which subsequently led to Gula introducing Paul to [[Sergio Toppi]]. **First link in a three-hop introduction chain**: Orbik → Gula → Toppi. The chain is itself a heritage artifact; Orbik's role in it is structural rather than only aesthetic.

American illustrator working primarily in the *noir-pulp-paperback tradition* — the hardboiled cover-art lineage, deeply influenced by painters like Robert McGinnis and the Dell/Pocket Books paperback-cover school of the 50s–70s. Highly skilled draftsman, compositionally strong, with a clear sense of dramatic low-key lighting. Active and teaching until his death in 2015.

## What Orbik's tradition adds to the visual axis

**Noir-pulp illustration** hasn't appeared elsewhere on Paul's map. It's a distinct and serious tradition with its own aesthetic priorities:

- **Dramatic low-key lighting** (chiaroscuro as atmosphere, not just modeling)
- **Figure-as-compositional-element** (the figure as a shape in a design, not just as a character in a scene)
- **Genre clarity over decoration** — the image has to do a specific narrative job in a small space; nothing is there that isn't earning its place
- **Physical presence and weight** — the tradition comes from selling a book by its cover; the image has to *feel* like something when you hold it

This is a different register from the fantasy-painter lineage (Frazetta, Horley, Brom), the European-draughtsmanship lineage (Moebius, Wendling), or the classical-atelier tradition (Gurney, Huston, Watts). It sits closer to the **American illustrator tradition** (overlapping with [[Howard Pyle and the Brandywine school]] in lineage) but specialized toward the genre-fiction-paperback economy of the mid-20th century.

## The introduction function

Orbik introduced Paul to [[Tim Gula]], who was a life-drawing instructor Paul then attended sessions with regularly. **Without Orbik, the Gula relationship — and consequently the Toppi discovery — might not have existed in this form.** The introduction is load-bearing for a significant downstream chain on the map.

That's the `introduction-chain-link` function: **a figure who is less important as a direct influence than as the vector through which other important connections were made.**

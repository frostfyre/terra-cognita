# Joey Ray Hall

**A huge presence at [[Blizzard Entertainment]]**, then **the person who hired Paul into [[Amazon Game Studios]].** Paul wanted to learn from him.

## The fourth named long-arc relationship

Joey Ray Hall completes a pattern that the [[high-trust-relationship-architecture]] concept predicted. The Amazon chapter had been faceless on the map; the pattern said it shouldn't be. **It isn't.**

This makes him at least the equivalent function in the Amazon chapter as [[Jeremy Cranford]] was for [[FTX Good Luck Games]] — the named human cause of an institutional move. The shape is now consistent across at least two chapters: **Paul follows individually-respected colleagues into new ventures.**

Function-tag observation: this isn't "Paul follows trusted people occasionally." It's **"Paul moves between studios primarily through trust relationships, not market-driven decisions."** That's a settled fact about how this career works. Update [[high-trust-relationship-architecture]] accordingly.

## Deliberately-chosen mentor-figure

The phrase Paul used: ***"I wanted to learn from him."***

That places Joey Ray Hall in a function-tag none of the other relationships occupy yet. Most of the named figures so far are friendships ([[John Scharmen]], [[Steve Aguilar]], [[Jeremy Cranford]]), peer colleagueships ([[Alex Horley]], [[Mark Gibbons]]), or lineage-instructors ([[James Gurney]], [[Steve Huston]], [[Bob Watts]], [[Tim Gula]], [[Nathan Fowkes]]). Joey Ray Hall is the **first explicitly-marked figure where Paul *chose to follow him in order to learn from him*.**

Active apprenticeship-orientation, not passive influence. Different category. The function-tag `deliberately-chosen-mentor-figure` should probably be applied retroactively to anyone else on the map Paul actively oriented toward as a teacher rather than absorbing as ambient influence.
